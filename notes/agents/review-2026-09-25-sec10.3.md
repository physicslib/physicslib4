## Findings (new this session)

### Medium

**M3. `lmm:hall-10.27`'s Lean proof independently bypasses the same
spectral-subspace/almost-eigenvector machinery its `\uses` list cites — and,
unlike the sibling case fixed for 10.23, this deviation is undocumented.**

- Location: `Physicslib4/Spectral/Unbounded/Normal.lean:764-882`
  (`exists_subspace_isAlmostEigenvector`, the Lean witness for
  `lmm:hall-10.27`); blueprint `unbounded-spectral-theorems.tex:2188-2216`.
- Problem: the blueprint's `\uses` for `lmm:hall-10.27` (line 2196) lists
  `def:hall-7.14, prpstn:hall-7.15, prpstn:hall-7.16, lmm:hall-10.25,
  lmm:hall-10.26` — the abstractly-defined spectral-subspace apparatus and
  the almost-eigenvector lemma just fixed above — and its proof text walks
  through "Step 1: $0\in\sigma(B^*B)$" via `lmm:hall-10.26`/`lmm:hall-10.25`,
  then "Step 2: the spectral subspace" via `def:hall-7.14`/`prpstn:hall-7.15`.
  Lean's `exists_subspace_isAlmostEigenvector`, by contrast (read in full,
  lines 777-900+), proves "$0\in\sigma(B^*B)$" by a direct unit argument
  (`Commute.isUnit_mul_iff` applied to `B` and `star B`, `Normal.lean:805-812`)
  that needs no almost-eigenvector reasoning at all, and realises the
  spectral subspace directly as `LinearMap.ker (cfc f T : H →L[ℂ] H)` for an
  explicit continuous `f` (`Normal.lean:813-857`), never calling
  `spectralSubspace`, `pvmOperator`, `mapsTo_pvmOperator_spectralSubspace`,
  `norm_sub_smul_le_of_mem_spectralSubspace`, `commute_borelCalculus`, or
  `mapsTo_spectralSubspace_of_commute` (the Lean names tagged to
  `def:hall-7.14`/`prpstn:hall-7.15`/`prpstn:hall-7.16`). It also never calls
  `exists_const_isAlmostEigenvector_mvApply`/`_uniform` (the witnesses for
  `lmm:hall-10.26`) or any Lean name tied to `lmm:hall-10.25`. Confirmed by
  `grep -rln` across the whole repo: none of those six spectral-subspace
  identifiers, nor the two almost-eigenvector-constant theorems, appear
  anywhere in `exists_subspace_isAlmostEigenvector`'s body, and the two
  almost-eigenvector-constant theorems have no caller anywhere in the repo
  at all (`grep -rln "exists_const_isAlmostEigenvector_mvApply"` returns only
  `Normal.lean`, at their own definition sites). This is the identical
  pattern to the now-fixed M1 (10.23 takes a shorter Mathlib-CFC route than
  its blueprint proof), but for a different declaration, and it did not
  receive the same documentation treatment: `spectrum_mvApply`'s docstring
  and the blueprint's `thrm:hall-10.23` both now carry an explicit
  "does not follow / formalization note" (and the 10.23 note even says, in
  passing, "Lemmas 10.25–10.27 are formalized in their own right" — implying
  each is faithfully proved to its *own* blueprint statement, which is true,
  but does not disclose that 10.27's *own* proof also diverges internally
  from what its own `\uses` list promises). No corresponding note exists on
  `exists_subspace_isAlmostEigenvector` or on `lmm:hall-10.27` in the `.tex`.
- Consequence for the audit: no soundness or statement-faithfulness issue —
  `exists_subspace_isAlmostEigenvector`'s signature matches
  `lmm:hall-10.27` exactly (checked: `∃ W : Submodule ℂ H, W ≠ ⊥ ∧
  IsClosed (W : Set H) ∧ (∀ ψ ∈ W, A ψ ∈ W) ∧ (∀ ψ ∈ W,
  ContinuousLinearMap.adjoint A ψ ∈ W) ∧ ∀ ψ ∈ W, ψ ≠ 0 →
  IsAlmostEigenvector (mvApply A p) ν ε ψ`, a **match**), and
  `#print axioms exists_subspace_isAlmostEigenvector` (tool-verified) gives
  only `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]`. The issue is purely that a
  reader using the blueprint's `\uses` graph to understand *how* the Lean
  development is wired together would be misled about this lemma too, in
  exactly the way M1 flagged for 10.23 — and the fix commit for M1
  (`2b06854`) had the opportunity to also flag this sibling case (it
  explicitly touches the surrounding text) but did not.
- Suggested fix (propose only): add a short docstring remark to
  `exists_subspace_isAlmostEigenvector`, and/or a blueprint "Formalization
  note" after `lmm:hall-10.27`'s proof, stating that the Lean proof
  constructs the witnessing subspace directly via `cfc` (as
  `ker (cfc f T)`) and via a direct unit/`Commute.isUnit_mul_iff` argument for
  $0\in\sigma(B^*B)$, rather than via the spectral-subspace definitions
  (`def:hall-7.14` etc.) or `lmm:hall-10.25`/`lmm:hall-10.26`, mirroring the
  note already present on `thrm:hall-10.23`.

### Low

**L3. `exists_const_isAlmostEigenvector_mvApply` (non-uniform) is now a
special case of `exists_const_isAlmostEigenvector_mvApply_uniform` but is
still proved by an independent ~85-line duplicate induction.**

- Location: `Physicslib4/Spectral/Unbounded/Normal.lean:503-585` (the
  original, non-uniform lemma) vs. `:593-685` (the uniform one added by
  `2b06854`). Both are still cited together in the blueprint's `\lean{}` tag
  for `lmm:hall-10.26` (line 2110), and both remain in the file.
- Problem: the two proofs are structurally identical (`step`/`mono`/`total`
  inductions on `MvPolynomial` support, `Normal.lean:508-546` vs.
  `600-640`), differing only in whether the bound on `‖lam‖` is fixed
  (`= ‖lam‖`, implicitly) or parametrized (`≤ R`). Since the non-uniform
  statement is a direct corollary of the uniform one (instantiate
  `R := ‖lam‖`), the ~85-line independent proof at lines 503-585 is
  redundant: it could be replaced by a 2-3 line application of
  `exists_const_isAlmostEigenvector_mvApply_uniform p ‖lam‖` composed with
  `le_refl`. Purely a maintenance/duplication cost (the kind of thing the
  task's "oversized proofs / duplication" hygiene check asks for); no
  soundness or statement issue — both theorems independently type-check with
  only the three standard axioms (tool-verified, see Method).
- Suggested fix (propose only): derive
  `exists_const_isAlmostEigenvector_mvApply` from
  `exists_const_isAlmostEigenvector_mvApply_uniform` (with `R := ‖lam‖`),
  removing the duplicate ~85-line proof. This changes an existing `theorem`
  body only (not a new declaration kind under the policy's block-list), but
  is still a real proof-body edit needing orchestrator sign-off, so left as
  a proposal.
