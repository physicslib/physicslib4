## Findings

### Medium — `IsPastEndpoint`/`IsFutureEndpoint` are typed for `SmoothPath`, contradicting the blueprint's explicit "no smoothness input" clause

**Location:** `Physicslib4/Spacetime/Curves.lean:920` and `:932`, cited by
`def:endpoints` (`spacetime.tex:270-281`).

The blueprint's `def:endpoints` states the past/future-endpoint notion for an
**arbitrary path**, and says so explicitly:

> "For an arbitrary path $\mu$, the past and future endpoints are singled out
> by extremality of the parameter, not by counting boundary components, and
> **no causal or smoothness input enters**: $p$ is a past endpoint of $\mu$
> if there is an $s \in \Sigma$ with $\mu(s) = p$ that is minimal..."
> (spacetime.tex:278, emphasis added)

The Lean declarations cited by this node are:

```
def IsPastEndpoint (μ : M.SmoothPath) (p : M.Carrier) : Prop :=
  ∃ s ∈ μ.parameterSpace, μ.toFun s = p ∧ (∀ s' ∈ μ.parameterSpace, s ≤ s')

def IsFutureEndpoint (μ : M.SmoothPath) (p : M.Carrier) : Prop :=
  ∃ s ∈ μ.parameterSpace, μ.toFun s = p ∧ (∀ s' ∈ μ.parameterSpace, s' ≤ s)
```

(`Curves.lean:920`, `:932`). Both take `μ : M.SmoothPath`, not `μ : M.Path`.
The companion `IsEndpoint (μ : M.Path) (p : M.Carrier) : Prop := ∃ s ∈
frontier μ.parameterSpace, μ.toFun s = p` (`Curves.lean:905`) is correctly
general — it is the two more specific declarations that narrow the domain.
Nothing in the *definition body* of `IsPastEndpoint`/`IsFutureEndpoint` uses
smoothness (only `parameterSpace`, `toFun`, and the order on `ℝ`, exactly as
the blueprint's own body says), so the restriction to `SmoothPath` is a type
signature choice, not a proof necessity: the same three-line body would
type-check verbatim for `μ : M.Path`.

This is a genuine, if practically narrow, faithfulness gap: it is Lean
*assuming more* (an extra structural hypothesis — smoothness plus
non-vanishing derivative — on the path) than the blueprint's own text, which
goes out of its way to disclaim exactly that hypothesis. It has no observed
downstream proof consequence, because every consumer of
`IsPastEndpoint`/`IsFutureEndpoint` in this codebase (`IsTripSegment`,
`IsCausalTripSegment` in `Causality.lean`, the pushforward lemmas in
`IsometryCausality.lean`, `DiffeoPath.lean`, `LorentzCausality.lean`, and the
Minkowski-specific corollaries in `Minkowski.lean:990-1040`) only ever
applies it to a `SmoothPath` — trips are built from smooth curves, so no
current theorem needs the general-`Path` form. But as a standalone
definition audited against its own blueprint entry, it is narrower than
stated, and the narrowing is not recorded anywhere (no `**Restriction:**`
line on `IsPastEndpoint`/`IsFutureEndpoint`, unlike the `IsGeodesic`
precedent the project asks agents to follow).

*Not a fix, but worth recording for whoever does fix it:* generalising
`IsPastEndpoint`/`IsFutureEndpoint` to `M.Path` would require re-deriving
`isEndpoint_of_isPastEndpoint`/`isEndpoint_of_isFutureEndpoint` and
`parameterSpace_eq_Icc_of_endpoints` for the general case (their proofs as
written only use `μ.toPath`, so this looks mechanical), and then providing a
`SmoothPath`-specific corollary for `IsTripSegment` to keep using.

### Low — `IsEndpoint`/`IsPastEndpoint`/`IsFutureEndpoint` have no `Curve`/`SmoothCurve`-level counterpart

`def:endpoints` says a point is an endpoint "of a path $\mu : \Sigma \to M$
**or its associated curve**" (spacetime.tex:271). Only the `Path`/`SmoothPath`
forms are formalized; there is no `Curve.IsEndpoint` or
`SmoothCurve.IsPastEndpoint` predicate defined via `Quot.lift` or an
existential-over-representative (the pattern used elsewhere in this file for
`IsTimelikeSmoothCurve`, `IsFutureOrientedSmoothCurve`, etc.). This is
consistent with how the definition is actually consumed downstream (`IsTrip`
always goes through a representative `SmoothPath` directly, never through a
free-standing curve-level endpoint predicate), so it is not blocking
anything, but the blueprint's "or its associated curve" clause has no direct
Lean witness. Low severity: a completeness gap, not a faithfulness error.

### Low — asymmetric strictness between `PathEquiv` and `SmoothPathEquiv`/`OrientedSmoothPathEquiv`

`PathEquiv` (`Curves.lean:264`) requires a **global** homeomorphism of `ℝ`
(`Function.LeftInverse`/`RightInverse` are stated unconditionally on all of
`ℝ`, not just on the parameter spaces) that happens to map `Σ₁` onto `Σ₂`.
`SmoothPathEquiv` and `OrientedSmoothPathEquiv` (`Curves.lean:279`, `:297`),
by contrast, only require `ContDiffOn`/mutual-inverse conditions **on the
respective parameter spaces**, with no requirement that `φ`, `ψ` extend to a
diffeomorphism of all of `ℝ`. Mathematically this is very likely harmless —
any homeomorphism (resp. diffeomorphism) between two non-degenerate closed
intervals of `ℝ` extends to one of all of `ℝ` — but the two definitions are
formalized at different levels of generality without comment, and the
blueprint's own wording ("a homeomorphism of the parameter space") is
uniform across `def:curves`'s two clauses where the Lean is not. Low
severity: a documentation/consistency point, not a known error, and it was
verified only by reading (no attempt was made to prove or disprove the
extension claim for the smooth case, which is the harder of the two).
