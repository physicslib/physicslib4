# Proposal: a Reeh–Schlieder section for Chapter 10

Status: D1–D4 approved as recommended (2026-11-07). The section is in the blueprint (`sec10/reeh-schlieder.tex`), reviewed and passed; not yet formalized. Log:
`reeh-schlieder-blueprint-progress.md`.

## Summary

Add a section proving the Reeh–Schlieder theorem for Haag–Kastler nets in Minkowski spacetime. The
section is organised as one hypothesis-free theorem and four corollaries.

- **Main theorem (no additivity hypothesis).** Let π be a representation of the quasilocal algebra
  and Ω a vector, with unitaries U implementing the translations on the vectors π(a)Ω and positive
  energy along every future-timelike direction. If small translates of a diamond B₁ stay inside a
  diamond B, then every vector π(A)Ω, for A in the algebra 𝔄_{B₁} generated by the translates of
  𝔘(B₁), lies in the closure of π(𝔘(B))Ω.

  Neither cyclicity of Ω nor any additivity property of the net is assumed.
- **Corollary A, under cyclicity for 𝔄.** If Ω is cyclic for π(𝔄_{B'}) for every diamond B', it is
  cyclic for π(𝔘(B)) for every diamond B. This is the minimal hypothesis, and it is checked per
  representation.
- **Corollary B, under weak additivity.** If the net is weakly additive (each 𝔄_{B'} is dense in
  𝔘) and Ω is cyclic for π(𝔘), then Ω is cyclic for every π(𝔘(B)). This is the textbook statement,
  with a hypothesis on the net alone.
- **Corollary C, under the von Neumann form.** If π(𝔄_{B'})'' contains π(𝔘) for every diamond B'
  and Ω is cyclic for π(𝔘), the same conclusion holds. The hypothesis depends on the
  representation but holds when weak-operator density holds and norm density fails.
- **Corollaries D and E.** D is the vacuum-state form of Corollary B; E says Ω is also separating
  for the local von Neumann algebras, through the existing `thrm:statistical-independence`.

The implications between the hypotheses are: weak additivity ⟹ von Neumann form ⟹ cyclicity
for 𝔄 (the last two given Ω cyclic for π(𝔘)). Corollary B follows from C, and C from A.

### Why the main theorem uses a smaller diamond

The proof needs room to translate: the function F in the proof outline vanishes on an *open* set
of translations only because small translates of B₁ stay inside B. With B₁ = B it vanishes only
at the origin, and analytic continuation needs more than that.

A statement with 𝔄_B for the same diamond B, "π(𝔘(B))Ω and π(𝔄_B)Ω have the same closure", would
need 𝔘(B) to be generated by the algebras of smaller diamonds inside it, which is again an
additivity property. The hypothesis-free theorem therefore relates B₁ to B, and the corollaries
quantify over all diamonds, which is harmless because every diamond contains smaller ones with
room to translate (`lmm:diamond-small-translates`).

### The analytic route

There is no joint spectral measure for the four translation generators. Analytic continuation is
done in one complex variable at a time, along one future-timelike direction at a time. This needs
only the library's per-direction positive energy (`IsPositiveEnergy`) and Stone's theorem in the
form `isPositiveEnergy_iff_exists_exp`.

## Gaps in the library to fill first

These are general facts, and each is a separate node below.

- **G1. Spectral support.** The spectral measure of a positive self-adjoint operator is supported
  on `[0, ∞)`. No such lemma was found in `Physicslib4/Spectral/Unbounded/`.
- **G2. Off-diagonal matrix elements.** `ProjectionValuedMeasure.inner_integral` gives
  `⟪ψ, (∫ f dE) ψ⟫ = ∫ f d(E.assoc ψ)` for the diagonal only. The off-diagonal `⟪χ, (∫ f dE) φ⟫`
  follows by polarization; it may be easier to work with polarized diagonal terms throughout.
- **G3. Holomorphy of the extension.** `z ↦ ∫_{[0,∞)} e^{izλ} dμ(λ)` is holomorphic for `Im z > 0`,
  continuous and bounded on `Im z ≥ 0`, for a finite measure μ. This is differentiation under the
  integral sign plus dominated convergence, both in Mathlib.
- **G4. Edge uniqueness.** A function holomorphic on the open upper half-plane, continuous up to the
  real axis, and zero on a real interval, is identically zero.
  - Proof: Schwarz reflection across the interval, then the identity theorem.
  - The reflection step reuses `differentiableOn_of_continuousOn_off_horizontal_line`
    (`Physicslib4/Analysis/HorizontalLineRemovable.lean`, with `ℓ = 0`).
  - No Schwarz reflection principle was found in Mathlib.
- **G5. Closure under the bicommutant.** For a unital \*-algebra S of bounded operators and any
  vector Ω, S Ω and S'' Ω have the same closure. The existing `lmm:cyclic-subspace-reduces` may
  supply most of it; check before adding a node.

## Proof outline of the main theorem

Let B₁ + x ⊆ B for every x in an open neighbourhood V of 0. Let K₀ be the closure of π(𝔘)Ω, and
let ψ be orthogonal to π(𝔘(B))Ω.

1. **Translations on K₀.** From U(x)π(a)Ω = π(β_x a)Ω:
   - with a = 1, U(x)Ω = Ω;
   - on K₀, U(x)π(a) = π(β_x a)U(x) and U(x + y) = U(x)U(y);
   - K₀ is invariant under π and U.

   No cyclicity is needed.
2. **Vanishing near the origin.** For B₁, …, Bₙ ∈ 𝔘(B₁), set
   `F(ξ₁, …, ξₙ) = ⟨ψ, U(ξ₁) π(B₁) U(ξ₂) π(B₂) ⋯ U(ξₙ) π(Bₙ) Ω⟩`.

   By step 1 this equals `⟨ψ, π(β_{x₁}B₁ ⋯ β_{xₙ}Bₙ) Ω⟩` with x_k = ξ₁ + ⋯ + ξ_k, and each
   factor lies in 𝔘(B₁ + x_k). If every x_k is in V, the product is in 𝔘(B), so F = 0 when each
   ξ_k is small.
3. **One variable, one direction.** With all variables but ξ_k fixed, F = ⟨χ, U(ξ_k) φ⟩ with φ in
   K₀. Along a future-timelike direction e, `t ↦ ⟨χ, U(te) U(v) φ⟩` extends holomorphically to the
   upper half-plane (by G1–G3), so by G4, vanishing for small t implies vanishing for all t.
4. **Spreading.** Vanishing on a nonempty open set W ⊆ ℝ⁴ spreads to W + ℝe for every
   future-timelike e. Four linearly independent future-timelike directions give
   W + ℝe₁ + ⋯ + ℝe₄ = ℝ⁴.
5. **All variables.** Apply step 4 to ξ₁ with the others small, then to ξ₂ with ξ₁ arbitrary and the
   rest small, and so on. Then F ≡ 0.
6. **Closure.** ψ is orthogonal to π(P)Ω for every P in the \*-algebra generated by the translates
   of 𝔘(B₁), and by continuity of π, for every P in its closure 𝔄_{B₁}. Since ψ was any vector
   orthogonal to π(𝔘(B))Ω, π(𝔄_{B₁})Ω lies in the closure of π(𝔘(B))Ω.

```latex
\section{The Reeh--Schlieder Theorem}\label{sctn:reeh-schlieder}

In a vacuum representation the vacuum vector is cyclic not only for the quasilocal algebra but for
the algebra of every diamond: states prepared by local operations in an arbitrarily small region
approximate every state of the theory. The section proves this in two stages. The main theorem,
which assumes no additivity property of the net, says that the vectors obtained from a diamond's
algebra already approximate those obtained from all translates of a smaller diamond's algebra.
Three corollaries then give cyclicity under successively stronger hypotheses: cyclicity for the
translation-generated algebras, the von Neumann form of weak additivity, and weak additivity
itself. The proof uses the spectrum condition through analytic continuation in one complex
variable at a time, along one future-timelike direction at a time.

\begin{definition}[Translation-Generated Algebra]
    \label{def:translation-generated-algebra}
    \uses{def:covariant-quasilocal-algebra, def:causal-diamond, def:future-timelike-translation}
    For a diamond $\mathbf{B}$, $\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbf{B}}$ is the C*-subalgebra of the quasilocal
    algebra $\mathfrak{U}$ generated by the translates
    $\iota_{\mathbf{B}+x}(\mathfrak{U}(\mathbf{B}+x))$, $x \in \mathbb{R}^4$: the norm closure of
    the $*$-algebra they generate.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Weak Additivity]
    \label{def:weak-additivity}
    \uses{def:translation-generated-algebra}
    A Haag--Kastler net is \emph{weakly additive} if $\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbf{B}} = \mathfrak{U}$ for
    every diamond $\mathbf{B}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Weak Additivity, von Neumann Form]
    \label{def:weak-additivity-von-neumann}
    \uses{def:translation-generated-algebra}
    A representation $\pi$ of $\mathfrak{U}$ satisfies \emph{weak additivity in the von Neumann
    sense} if $\pi(\mathfrak{U}) \subseteq \pi(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbf{B}})''$ for every diamond
    $\mathbf{B}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Weak Additivity Implies the von Neumann Form]
    \label{lmm:weak-additivity-von-neumann}
    \uses{def:weak-additivity, def:weak-additivity-von-neumann}
    If the net is weakly additive, every representation satisfies weak additivity in the von
    Neumann sense.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    $\pi(\mathfrak{U}) = \pi(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbf{B}}) \subseteq \pi(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbf{B}})''$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Translates of a Smaller Diamond]
    \label{lmm:diamond-small-translates}
    \uses{def:causal-diamond}
    For every diamond $\mathbf{B}$ there are a diamond $\mathbf{B}_1$ and an open neighbourhood $V$
    of $0$ in $\mathbb{R}^4$ with $\mathbf{B}_1 + x \subseteq \mathbf{B}$ for every $x \in V$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    Write $\mathbf{B} = I^+(p) \cap I^-(q)$ and choose $p \ll p' \ll q' \ll q$. Take
    $\mathbf{B}_1 = I^+(p') \cap I^-(q')$; its translate by $x$ is $I^+(p'+x) \cap I^-(q'+x)$. Since
    $I^+(p)$ and $I^-(q)$ are open, $p \ll p' + x$ and $q' + x \ll q$ for $x$ near $0$, and then
    $\mathbf{B}_1 + x \subseteq \mathbf{B}$ by transitivity of $\ll$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Spectral Support of a Positive Operator]
    \label{lmm:positive-spectral-support}
    The spectral measure $E$ of a positive self-adjoint operator $A$ satisfies
    $E((-\infty, 0)) = 0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    For $\psi$ in the range of $E((-n, -1/n))$, $\psi$ is in the domain of $A$ and
    $\langle \psi, A\psi \rangle \le -\|\psi\|^2/n$, contradicting positivity unless $\psi = 0$.
    Take the union over $n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Holomorphic Extension under Positive Energy]
    \label{lmm:positive-energy-holomorphic}
    \uses{def:positive-energy, thrm:positive-energy-iff-exp, lmm:positive-spectral-support}
    Let $V$ be a one-parameter unitary group with positive energy and let $\chi, \varphi \in H$.
    Then $t \mapsto \langle \chi, V(t)\varphi \rangle$ is the restriction to $\mathbb{R}$ of a
    function that is holomorphic on $\{\operatorname{Im} z > 0\}$ and bounded and continuous on
    $\{\operatorname{Im} z \ge 0\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    By Stone's theorem $V(t) = e^{itA}$ with $A$ positive and self-adjoint. By polarization,
    $\langle \chi, V(t)\varphi \rangle$ is a combination of integrals
    $\int_{[0,\infty)} e^{it\lambda} \, d\mu(\lambda)$ against finite positive measures
    (Lemma~\ref{lmm:positive-spectral-support}). Replace $t$ by $z$: for $\operatorname{Im} z \ge 0$
    the integrand is bounded by $1$, so the integral is bounded and continuous by dominated
    convergence, and holomorphic for $\operatorname{Im} z > 0$ by differentiation under the
    integral sign.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Vanishing on an Edge]
    \label{lmm:edge-vanishing}
    A function holomorphic on the open upper half-plane, continuous on its closure, and zero on an
    open interval of the real axis is identically zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    Extend the function to the lower half-plane by $f(z) = \overline{f(\bar z)}$. The extension is
    continuous on a neighbourhood of the interval and holomorphic off the real axis, hence
    holomorphic there. It vanishes on the interval, so it vanishes on the connected upper
    half-plane by the identity theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Spreading Along Timelike Directions]
    \label{lmm:timelike-spreading}
    \uses{lmm:positive-energy-holomorphic, lmm:edge-vanishing, def:future-timelike-translation}
    Let $U$ be unitaries on $H$ indexed by the translations $\mathbb{R}^4$, and $K_0 \subseteq H$ a
    closed subspace invariant under every $U(x)$, on which $U(x+y) = U(x)U(y)$. Suppose every
    future-timelike translation subgroup has positive energy. If $\chi \in H$, $\varphi \in K_0$,
    and $x \mapsto \langle \chi, U(x)\varphi \rangle$ vanishes on a nonempty open set, it vanishes
    identically.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    If it vanishes on an open $W$, then for $v \in W$ and $e$ future timelike, the function
    $t \mapsto \langle \chi, U(te) U(v)\varphi \rangle$ vanishes for small $t$, hence for all $t$
    (Lemmas~\ref{lmm:positive-energy-holomorphic} and~\ref{lmm:edge-vanishing}). So it vanishes on
    $W + \mathbb{R}e$. Four linearly independent future-timelike directions give
    $W + \mathbb{R}e_1 + \dots + \mathbb{R}e_4 = \mathbb{R}^4$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Translations on the Cyclic Subspace]
    \label{lmm:covariant-translations-cyclic-subspace}
    \uses{def:covariant-quasilocal-algebra}
    Let $\pi$ be a representation of $\mathfrak{U}$ on $H$, $\Omega \in H$, and $U(x)$ unitaries
    with $U(x)\pi(a)\Omega = \pi(\beta_x a)\Omega$ for all translations $x$ and all $a$. Let $K_0$
    be the closure of $\pi(\mathfrak{U})\Omega$. Then $U(x)\Omega = \Omega$; $K_0$ is invariant
    under every $\pi(a)$ and $U(x)$; and on $K_0$, $U(x)\pi(a) = \pi(\beta_x a)U(x)$ and
    $U(x+y) = U(x)U(y)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    Take $a = 1$ for invariance of $\Omega$. The identities hold on the vectors $\pi(b)\Omega$
    because $\beta$ is a group action by $*$-automorphisms, for instance
    $U(x)\pi(a)\pi(b)\Omega = \pi(\beta_x(ab))\Omega = \pi(\beta_x a)U(x)\pi(b)\Omega$, and extend
    to $K_0$ by continuity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Reeh--Schlieder]
    \label{thrm:reeh-schlieder}
    \uses{def:translation-generated-algebra, lmm:timelike-spreading,
      lmm:covariant-translations-cyclic-subspace, def:local-von-neumann}
    Let $\pi$ be a representation of $\mathfrak{U}$ on $H$, $\Omega \in H$, and $U(x)$ unitaries
    with $U(x)\pi(a)\Omega = \pi(\beta_x a)\Omega$ for all translations $x$ and all $a$, such that
    every future-timelike translation subgroup has positive energy. Let $\mathbf{B}_1$ and
    $\mathbf{B}$ be diamonds with $\mathbf{B}_1 + x \subseteq \mathbf{B}$ for every $x$ in a
    neighbourhood of $0$. Then $\pi(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbf{B}_1})\Omega$ lies in the closure of
    $\pi(\mathfrak{U}(\mathbf{B}))\Omega$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Let $\psi$ be orthogonal to $\pi(\mathfrak{U}(\mathbf{B}))\Omega$. For
    $B_1, \dots, B_n \in \mathfrak{U}(\mathbf{B}_1)$, the function
    $F(\xi_1, \dots, \xi_n) = \langle \psi, U(\xi_1)\pi(B_1)U(\xi_2)\pi(B_2) \cdots U(\xi_n)\pi(B_n)\Omega \rangle$
    equals $\langle \psi, \pi(\beta_{x_1}B_1 \cdots \beta_{x_n}B_n)\Omega \rangle$ with
    $x_k = \xi_1 + \dots + \xi_k$
    (Lemma~\ref{lmm:covariant-translations-cyclic-subspace}), so it vanishes when every $x_k$ is
    small. Applying Lemma~\ref{lmm:timelike-spreading} to one variable at a time, with the earlier
    variables arbitrary and the later ones small, shows $F \equiv 0$. Hence $\psi$ is orthogonal to
    $\pi(P)\Omega$ for every $P$ in the $*$-algebra generated by the translates of
    $\mathfrak{U}(\mathbf{B}_1)$, and by continuity for every $P \in \mathfrak{A}_{\mathbf{B}_1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Reeh--Schlieder under Cyclicity for the Translation-Generated Algebras]
    \label{crllr:reeh-schlieder-cyclic}
    \uses{thrm:reeh-schlieder, lmm:diamond-small-translates}
    Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thrm:reeh-schlieder} on $\pi$, $\Omega$ and $U$, if
    $\Omega$ is cyclic for $\pi(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbf{B}'})$ for every diamond $\mathbf{B}'$, then
    $\Omega$ is cyclic for $\pi(\mathfrak{U}(\mathbf{B}))$ for every diamond $\mathbf{B}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
    Choose $\mathbf{B}_1$ for $\mathbf{B}$ by Lemma~\ref{lmm:diamond-small-translates}. The closure
    of $\pi(\mathfrak{U}(\mathbf{B}))\Omega$ contains $\pi(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbf{B}_1})\Omega$ by
    Theorem~\ref{thrm:reeh-schlieder}, which is dense.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Closure of a Bicommutant Orbit]
    \label{lmm:bicommutant-orbit-closure}
    \uses{lmm:cyclic-subspace-reduces}
    For a unital $*$-algebra $S$ of bounded operators on $H$ and $\Omega \in H$, the vectors
    $S\Omega$ and $S''\Omega$ have the same closure.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    The projection $P$ onto the closure of $S\Omega$ commutes with $S$, since the closure is
    invariant under the $*$-closed set $S$; so $P \in S'$. For $T \in S''$,
    $T\Omega = TP\Omega = PT\Omega$ lies in the range of $P$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Reeh--Schlieder under the von Neumann Form of Weak Additivity]
    \label{crllr:reeh-schlieder-von-neumann}
    \uses{crllr:reeh-schlieder-cyclic, def:weak-additivity-von-neumann,
      lmm:bicommutant-orbit-closure}
    Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thrm:reeh-schlieder} on $\pi$, $\Omega$ and $U$, if $\pi$
    satisfies weak additivity in the von Neumann sense and $\Omega$ is cyclic for $\pi(\mathfrak{U})$,
    then $\Omega$ is cyclic for $\pi(\mathfrak{U}(\mathbf{B}))$ for every diamond $\mathbf{B}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
    By Lemma~\ref{lmm:bicommutant-orbit-closure}, the closure of
    $\pi(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbf{B}'})\Omega$ contains $\pi(\mathfrak{A}_{\mathbf{B}'})''\Omega$, hence
    $\pi(\mathfrak{U})\Omega$, which is dense. Apply
    Corollary~\ref{crllr:reeh-schlieder-cyclic}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Reeh--Schlieder under Weak Additivity]
    \label{crllr:reeh-schlieder-weak-additivity}
    \uses{crllr:reeh-schlieder-von-neumann, lmm:weak-additivity-von-neumann}
    Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thrm:reeh-schlieder} on $\pi$, $\Omega$ and $U$, if the
    net is weakly additive and $\Omega$ is cyclic for $\pi(\mathfrak{U})$, then $\Omega$ is cyclic
    for $\pi(\mathfrak{U}(\mathbf{B}))$ for every diamond $\mathbf{B}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
    Lemma~\ref{lmm:weak-additivity-von-neumann} and
    Corollary~\ref{crllr:reeh-schlieder-von-neumann}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Reeh--Schlieder for a Vacuum State]
    \label{crllr:reeh-schlieder-vacuum}
    \uses{crllr:reeh-schlieder-weak-additivity, def:vacuum-state-concrete}
    If the net is weakly additive, $\omega$ is a vacuum state, and $(K, \pi, \Omega, U)$ is a
    representative of $\omega$ with $\Omega$ cyclic for $\pi(\mathfrak{U})$, then $\Omega$ is cyclic
    for every local algebra.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
    The vacuum conditions restricted to translations are the hypotheses of
    Theorem~\ref{thrm:reeh-schlieder}; apply
    Corollary~\ref{crllr:reeh-schlieder-weak-additivity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The Vacuum is Separating]
    \label{crllr:reeh-schlieder-separating}
    \uses{crllr:reeh-schlieder-cyclic, thrm:statistical-independence}
    Under the hypotheses of Corollary~\ref{crllr:reeh-schlieder-cyclic}, $\Omega$ is separating for
    the local von Neumann algebra $R(\mathbf{B}_2)$ of every diamond $\mathbf{B}_2$ that is spacelike
    to some diamond $\mathbf{B}_1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
    $\Omega$ is cyclic for $\pi(\mathfrak{U}(\mathbf{B}_1))$ by
    Corollary~\ref{crllr:reeh-schlieder-cyclic}; apply Theorem~\ref{thrm:statistical-independence}.
\end{proof}
```

## Notes for the formalizer

- **Translations.** Use `translationSub` and `isAlexandrovBasisSet_smul` for translates of
  diamonds. 𝔄_B can be the topological closure of
  `StarSubalgebra.adjoin ℂ (⋃ x, Set.range (N.quasilocal.ι (isAlexandrovBasisSet_smul (translationSub x) hB)))`,
  or be stated through `N.action (translationSub x)` and `action_ι`.
- **Cyclicity.** Local cyclicity is `Dense ((fun T => T Ω) '' N.localOperators π hB)`, the form
  `localVonNeumann_separating` already takes, so Corollary E is a direct application.
- **The von Neumann form.** It uses the library's commutant convention, `Set.centralizer` in the
  bounded operators.
- **Lemma sizes.** The analytic lemmas (`lmm:positive-energy-holomorphic`, `lmm:edge-vanishing`)
  are the largest. Each may need splitting further when formalized, following the chapter's rule of
  short proofs.
- **Restrictions.** The section needs no `**Restriction:**` line: the theorem is stated in full
  generality for Minkowski nets, and the additivity hypotheses belong to the corollaries, as in the
  standard theory.
