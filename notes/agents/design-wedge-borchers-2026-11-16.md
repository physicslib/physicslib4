# Design memo: wedges, wedge algebras, the vacuum Poincaré implementation and the target theorem (2026-11-16)

Context: `notes/agents/scout-bisognano-wichmann-2026-11-16.md`. The aim is Bisognano–Wichmann (BW)
inside Haag–Kastler, without Wightman fields, in three stages (Q14):
- **stage 1:** BW-a, BW up to an internal symmetry under modular covariance, through Borchers'
  theorem and its wedge corollary;
- **stage 2:** the Brunetti–Guido–Longo theorem;
- **stage 3:** the algebraic PCT theorem.

This memo proposes; it decides nothing. Region classes, the fields of axiom structures and new
definitions are design decisions for the user.

Facts about the library this memo relies on:
- **Regions.** Diamonds are `IsAlexandrovBasisSet` (the empty set included). The net axioms
  (`LocalNet`, `Isotony`, `HaagKastlerNet`) are indexed by diamonds only.
- **Local algebras.** `localVonNeumann π hB` is the set π(𝔘(B))″. `localVonNeumannAlgebra` is
  the same object as a Mathlib `VonNeumannAlgebra H`. Isotony is `localVonNeumann_mono`.
- **The Poincaré group.** `InhomogeneousLorentzGroup` is the *restricted* group: SO(1,3)↑ ⋉ ℝ⁴,
  with no reflections.
- **Vacuum data.**
  - `IsVacuumState`, `reeh_schlieder_cyclic_of_vacuum` and the separating form take
    `U : InhomogeneousLorentzGroup → (H ≃ₗᵢ[ℂ] H)`.
  - Implementation is required only on vectors: `U L (π a Ω) = π (β_L a) Ω`.
  - The spectrum condition is `IsPositiveEnergy (fun t => U (γ t))` for *future-timelike*
    translation subgroups γ (`IsFutureTimelikeTranslation`, open forward cone).
  - No group law, no continuity in L, and no lightlike directions are required.
- **Modular theory** is stated for `M : Set (H →L[ℂ] H)` with `IsVonNeumannSet M`.

## Q2. Do wedges enter the axioms, or only as an index for derived algebras?

**Option 2a: derived only (recommended).**
- The net axioms stay diamond-indexed.
- Wedges are a predicate on subsets of spacetime, used only to index algebras built from the
  diamond algebras (Q4).
- Pros:
  - No change to `LocalNet`, `Isotony` or `HaagKastlerNet`.
  - Consistent with the recorded decision that the library attaches no algebra to unbounded
    regions at the level of the net. The wedge algebra is a von Neumann algebra in a
    representation, not a C*-algebra in the net.
  - Agreement with R(B) on diamonds is a two-line lemma (§Q4).
- Cons:
  - The wedge algebra exists only in a representation, not abstractly.
  - Results phrased for abstract C*-nets indexed by wedges do not apply directly. Borchers and
    BW do not need them.

**Option 2b: a second region class in the axioms.**
- `LocalNet` gets wedge-indexed C*-algebras 𝔘(W) with isotony between diamonds and wedges.
- Pros: closer to treatments that start from wedge-local nets (Borchers, Brunetti–Guido–Longo).
- Cons:
  - Changes the central axiom structures.
  - Needs compatibility conditions (𝔘(W) generated by the 𝔘(B) with B ⊆ W), which re-derive 2a
    anyway.
  - Affects every existing chapter.

**Option 2c: a separate wedge-local framework.**
- A parallel structure "net of von Neumann algebras on wedges", in the style of nets of standard
  subspaces (Brunetti–Guido–Longo 2002, Morinelli).
- Pros: the cleanest home for modular-covariance results.
- Cons:
  - A second framework to maintain.
  - Bridging back to `HaagKastlerNet` is itself Option 2a.

**Recommendation: 2a.** The wedge predicate:

  `IsWedge (W : Set StandardMinkowskiSpacetime.Carrier) : Prop := ∃ g : InhomogeneousLorentzGroup, W = g • W_R`

with W_R := {x | |x⁰| < x¹} the open right wedge. Minkowski only.

## Q4. How general is the derived algebra?

**Option 4a: the algebra of an arbitrary region (recommended).**

  `regionVonNeumann π O := Set.centralizer (Set.centralizer (⋃ B, ⋃ (hB : IsAlexandrovBasisSet B) (_ : B ⊆ O), localVonNeumann π hB))`

The wedge algebra R(W) is `regionVonNeumann π W` for `IsWedge W`.

- Plain inclusion B ⊆ O, not closure ⊆ O. With plain inclusion, R(B) agrees with
  `regionVonNeumann π B` on every diamond B: ⊇ because B ⊆ B, ⊆ by isotony plus R(B)″ = R(B).
  The strict variant would turn this into an inner-continuity assumption.
- Pros:
  - One definition covers wedges, their causal complements W′ (themselves wedges), double cones
    and the unions used for additivity.
  - It agrees with `localVonNeumann` on diamonds.
  - It is monotone in O.
  - It fits the form of `IsWeaklyAdditiveVonNeumann`, which already speaks of bicommutants of
    unions of translates.
- Cons:
  - For regions containing no nonempty diamond, it is ℂ1 (the empty diamond gives ℂ1). Harmless.
  - Its name and docstring must stress that it is a derived object, not an axiom.

**Option 4b: wedge-only.** The same formula, restricted to `IsWedge W` by a hypothesis argument.
It is simpler to read, but the causal complement and the compatibility lemma would need their
own definitions later.

**Option 4c: generated by translates.** R(W) := (⋃_{a ∈ W} R(B₀ + a))″ for a fixed diamond
family. This links to weak additivity but depends on choices; not recommended.

**Representation and packaging (Q5, Q6).**
- Parametrize by any `π`, as `localVonNeumann` is.
- Use the `Set` form, since modular theory is `Set`-based. Add a bundled
  `VonNeumannAlgebra H` version only if a caller needs it, via the existing
  `vonNeumannOfSelfAdjoint`. The union is self-adjoint because each `localVonNeumann` is.

**Recommendation: 4a, as a `Set` in any representation.** The first lemmas:
- (i) `IsVonNeumannSet (regionVonNeumann π O)`;
- (ii) agreement with `localVonNeumann` on diamonds;
- (iii) monotonicity;
- (iv) covariance U(g) R(O) U(g)* = R(gO), once Q8 supplies operator-level implementation;
- (v) for a wedge W, locality R(W′) ⊆ R(W)′, which needs Q7;
- (vi) Ω cyclic and separating for R(W) in the vacuum representation:
  - cyclicity from Reeh–Schlieder, since W contains a nonempty diamond;
  - separation from cyclicity for R(W′) ⊆ R(W)′.

## Q8. How is the unitary Poincaré implementation provided?

Borchers' theorem and its wedge corollary need more than the current vacuum data:
- **Operator-level implementation:** U(g) π(a) U(g)* = π(β_g a), not only on the vectors π(a)Ω.
  This is needed for U(g) R(O) U(g)* = R(gO).
- **Invariance:** U(g)Ω = Ω.
- **The group law:** U(gh) = U(g)U(h), so that translations and boosts compose:
  U(Λ)U(a)U(Λ)* = U(Λa).
- **Strong continuity of the subgroups used:** lightlike and transverse translations, and boosts.
- **Positive energy along future lightlike directions:** see Q10.

**Option 8a: a hypothesis structure on vacuum data (recommended).** A new `structure`, e.g.
`IsCovariantVacuumRep N π Ω U : Prop`, with fields:
- a group law;
- strong continuity, of the whole map or of each one-parameter subgroup;
- `∀ g a, U g ∘L π a ∘L (U g)⁻¹ = π (N.action g a)`;
- `∀ g, U g Ω = Ω`;
- positive energy for future-causal translation directions (Q10);
- cyclicity of Ω.

It is passed as a hypothesis, as `IsVacuumState` is today.

Pros:
- No change to the axiom structures.
- It sits next to `IsVacuumState`, which a lemma can show it refines: the operator-level form
  gives the vector-level form.
- Each field is a standard assumption.

Cons: it overlaps with `IsVacuumState`, so a bridge lemma is needed, and a clear docstring on why
both exist.

**Option 8b: strengthen `IsVacuumState`.** Add the operator-level implementation, the group law
and continuity to the existing definition.
- Pros: one notion of vacuum.
- Cons:
  - It changes a definition already used by Reeh–Schlieder.
  - Those theorems then assume more than they need, unless they keep the weaker binders, which
    they mostly do.

**Option 8c: a field of `HaagKastlerNet` or a "vacuum net" structure.**
- Pros: closest to textbook "a net with a vacuum representation".
- Cons:
  - Changes the central axiom structure.
  - Forces a preferred representation into the net, against the current representation-agnostic
    design.

**Recommendation: 8a**, with a lemma `IsCovariantVacuumRep → IsVacuumState`-style data.

**Restriction:** strong continuity can be required of the whole map, or only of the
one-parameter subgroups. Asking only for the subgroups the proofs use (translation flows,
boosts) keeps the hypothesis minimal. Full continuity on the group is the textbook form and can
be recorded as the intended general form.

## Q14. What is the target theorem? (BW-a, then BGL, then PCT)

The target is **a conditional BW theorem inside Haag–Kastler, without Wightman fields**, reached
through Borchers' theorem. It comes in three layers, each a theorem in its own right.

### Layer 1: Borchers' theorem (unconditional, pure operator algebra)

Let M be a von Neumann algebra with Ω cyclic and separating, and V(t) = e^{itP} with P ≥ 0,
V(t)Ω = Ω and V(t)MV(t)* ⊆ M for t ≥ 0. Then:
- Δ^{is}V(t)Δ^{−is} = V(e^{−2πs}t);
- JV(t)J = V(−t).

Source: Borchers, CMP 143 (1992); simpler proofs by Florig (1998) and in Longo's notes.

### Layer 2: the wedge corollary (unconditional, needs Q2, Q4 and Q8)

For every translation a:
- Δ_W^{it}U(a)Δ_W^{−it} = U(Λ_W(−2πt)a);
- J_W U(a) J_W = U(j_W a).

Hence Z(t) := Δ_W^{it}U(Λ_W(2πt)):
- commutes with every U(a);
- fixes Ω;
- maps R(W) onto itself.

BW is the statement Z ≡ 1.

### Layer 3 (headline, stage 1): BW-a, Bisognano–Wichmann up to an internal symmetry

**Hypothesis.** One, algebraic, and required by the counterexamples (Yngvason, "A note on
essential duality"; Borchers; Morinelli 2018):

- **(MC) Modular covariance for the wedge W.** For every t and every diamond B:

    Δ_W^{it} R(B) Δ_W^{−it} = R(Λ_W(−2πt)B).

  The modular group of R(W) moves local algebras the way the boosts do. This is an equality of
  sets of operators: it does not assert Δ_W^{it} = U(Λ_W(−2πt)).

With the given covariant U of Q8, (MC) for one wedge gives (MC) for every wedge gW. The reason is
that U(g) maps R(W) onto R(gW) and fixes Ω, so U(g)Δ_W^{it}U(g)* = Δ_{gW}^{it} (the Q20 lemma,
for a unitary mapping one algebra onto another). BGL (§ Findings) assume (MC) for all wedges and
no given U.

**Theorem BW-a.** Under (MC), for every t:
- Δ_W^{it} = Z(t) U(Λ_W(−2πt));
- Z(t) fixes Ω;
- Z(t) R(B) Z(t)* = R(B) for every diamond B, so Z(t) is an internal symmetry;
- Z(t) commutes with every translation U(a).

Also J_W U(a) J_W = U(j_W a), from Layer 2.

**Proof.** Write Z(t) = Δ_W^{it}U(Λ_W(2πt)).
- Z(t)Ω = Ω, because both factors fix Ω.
- Z(t)R(B)Z(t)* = Δ_W^{it}R(Λ_W(2πt)B)Δ_W^{−it} = R(B), by operator-level covariance (Q8) and
  then (MC).
- Z(t)U(a)Z(t)* = U(a), by the group law (Q8) and then the wedge corollary.

**Additional properties (cheap, worth stating):**
- Z(t) commutes with every Δ_{W′}^{is} and J_{W′}, by the Q20 lemma: a unitary fixing Ω and
  normalizing every R(B) normalizes every R(W′).
- Z(t) commutes with U(Λ_W(s)), since U(Λ_W(s)) preserves R(W) and Ω and so commutes with
  Δ_W^{it}.
- Hence t ↦ Z(t) is a one-parameter group, as in BGL's proof of Theorem 2.3.

**BW-a excludes no theory.** Nets with internal symmetries (charged fields, several copies, tensor
products with the swap) satisfy it, with Z(t) inside their internal symmetry group. It says
exactly what can go wrong: only an internal symmetry separates Δ_W^{it} from the boost.

**(NI) is dropped.** It excluded nets for which Bisognano–Wichmann holds:
- the net of a free complex scalar field, with U(1) acting non-trivially and Ω invariant;
- n copies of a free field (O(n));
- A ⊗ A (the swap).

Note on electromagnetism. In the Doplicher–Haag–Roberts picture the Haag–Kastler net is the net of
gauge-invariant observables, and a global charge symmetry acts trivially on its vacuum sector. So
QED's U(1) did not in fact violate (NI). The examples above did.

**Docstring restriction line for BW-a.** "**Restriction:** assumes modular covariance for the
wedge (MC) and identifies Δ_W^{it} with the boost only up to an internal symmetry Z(t). The intended
form, exact Bisognano–Wichmann for a covariance representation, is Brunetti–Guido–Longo's
Theorem 2.3 (stage 2), not yet formalized."

### Stage 2: the Brunetti–Guido–Longo theorem (BW for the modular representation)

This is BGL, Theorem 2.3 (Rev. Math. Phys. 7 (1995) 57–71; arXiv funct-an/9401002), verified
against the paper; see Findings below. Its hypotheses are (MC) for all wedges, a local net on
double cones, Ω cyclic, and dim M > 2. It needs no given representation.

It concludes that there is a vacuum-preserving, positive-energy, covariant, strongly continuous
unitary representation U_mod of the universal cover P̃ of P₊↑, canonically determined by
U_mod(Λ̃_W(t)) = Δ_W^{it} for every wedge W. This is exact Bisognano–Wichmann for U_mod.

**Uniqueness (Corollary 2.8).** Under the distal split property, U_mod is the only covariant
representation of P̃. So with distal split, a given covariant U (lifted to P̃) equals U_mod, and
exact Bisognano–Wichmann holds for U. This is the physically standard conditional form, and it
replaces (NI) by split, which holds in the free theories that (NI) excluded.

**Cost.** The ingredients, from the paper:
- **(a) Proposition 2.2, essential duality** R(W)′ = R(W′) under (MC), cited to BGL 1993 [4],
  Theorem 2.3(i).
- **(b) Proposition 2.4:** the boosts generate the Lie algebra of the Poincaré group, so they
  algebraically generate P₊↑.
- **(c) Lemma 2.5:** every nontrivial normal subgroup of P₊↑ contains the translations. It uses
  the Lorentz orbits in ℝⁿ. The degenerate alternative is that all local algebras coincide and are
  maximal abelian.
- **(d) Lemma 2.6:** the group G generated by all wedge modular groups is a central extension of
  P₊↑. It uses that unitaries normalizing all wedge algebras and fixing Ω commute with the modular
  groups, which is the Q20 lemma.
- **(e) Corollary 1.8 (the cohomological core):** a central "weak Lie" extension of P₊↑ by a
  unitary group splits on P̃. It rests on Theorem 1.7, which generalizes Moore's measurable
  cohomology of locally compact groups by Polish groups (Theorem 1.3, Moore TAMS 221, 1976), plus
  perfectness of P₊↑ and H²(𝔭, ℝ) = 0.
- **(f) z(t) ≡ 1:** the internal one-parameter group z(t) = Δ_W^{it}U_W(−t) is central, does not
  depend on W, and z(t) = z(−t) by essential duality, so z ≡ 1.
- **(g) Proposition 2.7, positive energy:** a converse of Borchers' theorem. It uses Borchers'
  Lemma II.3, the analytic continuation of U(e^{−2πt}a) to the strip −1/2 < Im z < 0 with norm
  at most 1, which Layer 1 needs anyway.
- **(h) The universal cover P̃** (ISL(2,ℂ)) with kernel ℤ₂. The library has only P₊↑ as a structure
  of linear maps. Descending from P̃ to P₊↑ is a spin–statistics question: BGL §3 defers it to the
  split property and an algebraic spin–statistics theorem.

**Estimate.**
- (e) dominates. Moore's measurable cohomology, Polish groups and measurable sections are far
  beyond Mathlib and the library: research-scale, plausibly several hundred nodes.
- (b), (c) and (h) are Lie-group infrastructure for SO(1,3)↑ and SL(2,ℂ), not in Mathlib in
  usable form: about 40–100 nodes.
- (a), (d), (f) and (g) reuse Layers 1–2 and Q20: about 20–40 nodes.

**Possible shortcut, to be scouted.** In our setting a covariant U is *given* (Q8), and BW-a gives
Δ_W^{it} ∈ U(P₊↑)·(internal symmetries). The splitting problem might then reduce to a cocycle with
values in the internal-symmetry group, avoiding Moore theory. Unverified; BGL's proof that
θ_g fixes z uses U ∈ G, which fails for a given U. A good scouting question before committing to
stage 2.

### Stage 3: the algebraic PCT theorem (antiunitary extension)

BGL §3 defers this to a forthcoming paper [7]: the relation between (MC) and the geometric action of
the wedge modular conjugations, "with applications to the construction of a PCT symmetry".
Candidates to scout:
- Guido–Longo, "An algebraic spin and statistics theorem", CMP 172 (1995);
- Guido–Longo, "Relativistic invariance and charge conjugation in quantum field theory",
  CMP 148 (1992), which is BGL's [9].

The target is to extend U_mod to the proper Poincaré group P₊ = P₊↑ ∪ P₊↓, with the
non-orthochronous component represented antiunitarily:
- J_W U_mod(g) J_W = U_mod(j_W g j_W);
- J_W R(O) J_W = R(j_W O);
- Θ := J_{W_R}U_mod(R₁(π)) a PCT operator.

Parity alone is never needed. **Cost:** a definition of P₊ and of "unitary-or-antiunitary
representation" (design decisions), plus about 40–80 nodes on top of the stage-2 group
infrastructure. Not sensible before stage 2.

### Recommendation

- **Stage 1 (commit now):** Layers 1–2 and BW-a, with (MC) as the only extra hypothesis. About
  60–100 nodes, with no group theory beyond the wedge boosts and translations.
- **Stage 2 (scout first):** BGL Theorem 2.3, and Corollary 2.8 for the given U under distal split.
  Before committing, scout the given-U shortcut and the size of a Moore-free proof of
  Corollary 1.8.
- **Stage 3 (after stage 2):** the algebraic PCT theorem, after scouting Guido–Longo 1995.
- **Blueprint statement:** unconditional Bisognano–Wichmann is false for general Haag–Kastler
  nets. BW-a states exactly the gap, and stage 2 closes it for the modular representation.

## Findings: Q7 and Q20 checked in the library (2026-11-16)

### Q7 — opposite-wedge locality

**The relation is pointwise, so closures play no role.**
- `Spacetime.IsCompletelySpacelike t O₁ O₂ := ∀ p₁ ∈ O₁, ∀ p₂ ∈ O₂, IsSpacelikeRelated t p₁ p₂`
  (`Spacetime/Causality.lean:300`).
- `IsSpacelikeRelated t p₁ p₂ := p₂ ∉ causalFuture t p₁ ∪ causalPast t p₁`.
- Diamonds inside the *open* wedges W_R and W_L = {x¹ < −|x⁰|} are completely spacelike exactly
  when every pair of points is spacelike related. The edge plays no role.
- Monotonicity (`isCompletelySpacelike_mono`) reduces this to IsCompletelySpacelike W_R W_L.

**Mathematically it holds.** For p ∈ W_R and q ∈ W_L:

  p¹ − q¹ > |p⁰| + |q⁰| ≥ |p⁰ − q⁰|,

so p − q is spacelike in the Minkowski form.

**Missing: the Minkowski characterization of causal precedence.**
- The library has the timelike/chronological side:
  - `segmentPrecedes_mem_minkowskiForwardCone`;
  - `standardMinkowski_trip_tangent_mem_minkowskiForwardCone_zero`;
  - `intervalIntegral_mem_minkowskiForwardCone_zero`;
  - `minkowskiForwardCone_subset_chronologicalFuture_standardMinkowski`.
- There is no causal analogue: no theorem that p ≼ q in standard Minkowski implies q − p lies in
  the *closed* forward cone.

**The causal analogue mirrors the timelike chain.**
- Causal trip segments are geodesic (`IsCausalTripSegment` carries the geodesic field), with
  future-causal tangents.
- So the tangent lies in the closed forward cone, the integral of the tangent lies in the closed
  cone (closed convex cone), and `CausallyPrecedes`, a reflexive-transitive closure, stays in the
  cone because the closed cone is closed under addition.
- Estimate: 4–6 lemmas, each modelled on an existing timelike one, plus a 1–2 line wedge lemma.

**Then locality follows** from `localVonNeumannAlgebra_le_commutant_of_subset_spacelikeComplement`
(`AQFT/HaagKastler/LocalVonNeumann.lean:181`) and the elementary fact that if A commutes
elementwise with B, then A″ ⊆ B′.

**Side remark.** `spacelikeComplement W_R` is the *closed* left wedge, since edge points are
spacelike to all of W_R. Every nonempty open diamond in it lies in the open wedge W_L, so
R(spacelikeComplement W_R) = R(W_L) under the Q4 definition. No closure issue arises there either.

**Verdict for Q7:**
- No design obstacle: Axiom 3 applies to diamonds in opposite wedges as stated.
- The only work is the missing Minkowski causal-cone lemma, a small, self-contained addition to
  `Spacetime/Minkowski.lean`.

### Q20 — unitaries preserving M and Ω commute with Δ^{it} and J

**Not in the library as a single lemma**, but the pieces exist:
- `modular_objects_independent_of_conjugation` (Modular.lean:233): Δ, Δ^{1/2} and J do not
  depend on the reference conjugation C.
- `eq_modularConjugation_of_continuous` (Modular.lean:205): J is the unique continuous K with
  K(Δ^{1/2}ψ) = Sψ.
- `UnitaryCommutes` together with `adjoint_commute_of_commute`, `adjointMulSelf_commute_of_commute`
  and `functionalCalculus_adjointMulSelf_commute_of_commute` (Spectral/Unbounded/Commuting.lean).
  A unitary commuting with c commutes with c†, with c†c, and with bounded real functions of c†c.
- `antiunitaryConj` with `functionalCalculus_antiunitaryConj`, `spectralMeasure_antiunitaryConj`
  and `pmapIntegral_antiunitaryConj` (Polar.lean). These transport the functional calculus along
  antiunitary conjugation, and a unitary V is (V∘C)∘C⁻¹, which
  `antiunitaryConj_trans_antiunitaryConj_of_commute` already exploits.
- `modularUnitaryGroup_eq_functionalCalculus` (VanDaele.lean), and `integral_eq_re_add_I_mul_im`
  (Polar.lean) for splitting complex bounded functions into real and imaginary parts.

**Proposed route**, with V unitary, V M V* = M and VΩ = Ω:
1. **V commutes with S₀ on MΩ.** V(xΩ) = (VxV*)Ω and S₀((VxV*)Ω) = V x* Ω, so it follows by
   closure that V commutes with S, including domains. *New:* a lemma that unitary conjugation
   commutes with closure (graph argument), unless one already exists in `Spectral/Unbounded/Basic`.
2. **V conjugates the linearized operator for C into the one for VCV*.** V S′_C V* = S′_{VCV*},
   since S′_C = C∘S and V S V* = S. VCV* is again an antiunitary involution.
3. **V commutes with Δ.**
   - Δ_C = S′_C† S′_C, so V Δ_C V* = (V S′_C V*)†(V S′_C V*) = Δ_{VCV*} = Δ_C, the last equality
     by independence of C. Hence `UnitaryCommutes V Δ`.
   - *New:* unitary conjugation commutes with the adjoint and with `adjointMulSelf`. This is a
     variant of the `Commuting.lean` lemmas for a conjugated operator, not an invariant one.
4. **V commutes with Δ^{it}.** Apply `functionalCalculus_adjointMulSelf_commute_of_commute` to the
   real and imaginary parts of e_t, namely cos(t log x) and sin(t log x) on (0, ∞), and combine.
   *New:* 1–2 lemmas.
5. **V commutes with Δ^{1/2}**, which is unbounded.
   - Either transport by `functionalCalculus_antiunitaryConj` applied twice (V = (VC)∘C⁻¹).
   - Or prove a general lemma: a unitary commuting with all spectral projections commutes with
     every `pmapIntegral`, since μ_{Vψ} = μ_ψ preserves `integralDomain`.
   - *New:* 1–2 lemmas.
6. **V commutes with J.** K := VJV* is continuous, and K(Δ^{1/2}ψ) = VJΔ^{1/2}V*ψ = VSV*ψ = Sψ, by
   steps 1 and 5. So K = J by `eq_modularConjugation_of_continuous`.

**Estimate:** 6–10 new lemmas. Two or three are general unbounded-operator facts, for
`Spectral/Unbounded/Commuting.lean`:
- unitary conjugation commutes with closure;
- unitary conjugation commutes with the adjoint and with c†c;
- a unitary commuting with a PVM commutes with its `pmapIntegral`.

The rest are short modular-theory corollaries. No new definitions are needed:
`UnitaryCommutes` exists.

**Verdict for Q20:**
- It follows from existing §10.10 and spectral results plus a handful of new lemmas.
- The cleanest lever is independence of the reference conjugation, applied to C and VCV*. It
  avoids the antilinearity of S entirely.

## Findings: the BGL paper scouted (2026-11-16)

Source: R. Brunetti, D. Guido, R. Longo, "Group cohomology, modular theory and space-time
symmetries", arXiv funct-an/9401002 (Rev. Math. Phys. 7 (1995) 57–71). The full PDF was read;
quotes are short.

**Setting (§2).**
- A local pre-cosheaf 𝒜 of von Neumann algebras on the double cones 𝒦 of Minkowski space M, with
  isotony and locality, on a *separable* Hilbert space.
- Ω is cyclic for every local algebra.
- The net is extended to open regions by additivity, and Reeh–Schlieder for regions with
  complement of nonempty interior is derived from locality.
- No Poincaré representation is assumed ("we do not have any given unitary representation of the
  Poincaré group from the start"). No positive energy, unique vacuum, irreducibility or split is
  assumed in Theorem 2.3.

**Wedges and conventions.**
- W₁ = {x : |x₀| < x₁}, and wedges are its Poincaré transforms.
- Λ_{W₁}(t) acts on (x₀, x₁) by the matrix with rows (cosh 2πt, −sinh 2πt) and
  (−sinh 2πt, cosh 2πt).
- So their Λ_W(t) is our Λ_W(−2πt) up to the sign convention; record this in the blueprint (Q15).

**Assumption 2.1 (modular covariance).** For *all* wedges W:
Δ_W^{it}𝒜(𝒪)Δ_W^{−it} = 𝒜(Λ_W(t)𝒪), for every 𝒪 ∈ 𝒦.

**Proposition 2.2.** Modular covariance implies essential duality 𝒜(W)′ = 𝒜(W′) for every wedge.
The proof is that of [4] (BGL, "Modular structure and duality in conformal QFT", CMP 156 (1993)),
Theorem 2.3(i). It does not use positive energy (footnote 1).

**Theorem 2.3.** "Let 𝒜 be a modular covariant, local pre-cosheaf on M, dim(M) > 2. Then there
exists a vacuum preserving, positive energy, 𝒜-covariant, unitary representation U of P̃ which is
canonically determined by the property U(Λ̃_W(t)) = Δ_W^{it}, W ∈ 𝒲." Here 𝒜-covariant means
U(g)𝒜(𝒪)U(g)* = 𝒜(σ(g)𝒪), with σ : P̃ → P₊↑ the covering map.

**Proof ingredients.**
- **Proposition 2.4:** the boosts generate the Lie algebra, hence P₊↑ algebraically.
- **Lemma 2.5:** H = {g : 𝒜(g𝒪) = 𝒜(𝒪) ∀𝒪} is normal. Either H = {1}, or every 𝒜(𝒪) equals
  𝒜₀ and is maximal abelian; the latter case is handled by the trivial representation.
- **Lemma 2.6:** G = ⟨modular groups⟩ is a central extension of P₊↑. The map π(Δ_W^{it}) = Λ_W(t)
  is well defined by Lemma 2.5, and ker π commutes with all modular groups (Strătilă–Zsidó).
- **Corollary 1.8:** weak Lie central extensions of P₊↑ split on P̃. It uses perfectness and
  H²(𝔭, ℝ) = 0 ("(4-dimensional)"), through Theorem 1.7, a generalization of Moore's measurable
  cohomology (Theorem 1.3, Moore TAMS 221 (1976)).
- **z(t) = Δ_W^{it}U_W(−t):** central in G, independent of W (θ_g acts trivially on the center and
  P₊↑ is transitive on wedges), a one-parameter group, and z(t) = z(−t) by essential duality.
  Hence z ≡ I.
- **Positive energy:** timelike generators are convex combinations of lightlike ones, and each
  lightlike translation gives a semigroup of endomorphisms of a wedge; then Proposition 2.7.

**Proposition 2.7 (converse of Borchers).** If ℛ is standard for Ω, and U(a), a ≥ 0, is a
one-parameter semigroup of endomorphisms of ℛ with U(a)Ω = Ω and Ad Δ^{it}U(a) = U(e^{−2πt}a), then U
has positive generator. The proof is from Borchers [2], Lemma II.3: analytic continuation to the
strip −1/2 < Im z < 0 with norm at most 1, evaluated at z = −i/4.

**Corollary 2.8.** "If the distal split property (cf. [6]) holds, the representation described in
Theorem 2.3 is the only 𝒜-covariant unitary representation of the universal covering group P̃ of
P₊↑ on ℋ." The proof is that of [4], Theorem 3.1. [6] is Doplicher–Longo, Invent. Math. 73 (1984).
The introduction adds that, for a covariant net, (MC) together with split forces the wedge modular
unitaries to equal the given boosts ([4]).

**§3 Final comments.**
- PCT and the geometric action of the modular conjugations are deferred to "a forthcoming paper
  [7]".
- The centre ℤ₂ of P̃ "might act as a non-trivial gauge symmetry". This is to be settled by an
  algebraic spin–statistics theorem, expected to follow from split.
- "Essential duality may fail in general", citing Yngvason [21].
- Borchers' theorem paper is [2]: "The CPT theorem in two-dimensional theories of local
  observables", CMP 143 (1992) 315.

**Consequences for the plan.**
- (MC) is the right single hypothesis. BW-a (stage 1) is BGL's internal-symmetry step, done
  relative to a given U without the group cohomology.
- Exact Bisognano–Wichmann for the *given* U needs Theorem 2.3 plus Corollary 2.8 with distal
  split, and so the Moore-theoretic Corollary 1.8. Stage 2's cost is dominated by Corollary 1.8 and
  the P̃ infrastructure.
- Stage 3 sources: BGL's [7] (the Guido–Longo papers of 1995), to scout.
