# Scout memo: what proving Bisognano–Wichmann within AQFT would take (2026-11-16)

This is exploration only: it changes no blueprint, Lean or website files. The literature
references are from memory and need checking against the sources before any of this becomes a
blueprint.

## 1. The statement, and why it is not a theorem of Haag–Kastler alone

**Bisognano–Wichmann (BW).** Let W_R = {x : x¹ > |x⁰|} be the right wedge and R(W_R) its von
Neumann algebra in the vacuum representation. Then Δ_W^{it} = U(Λ_W(−2πt)), where Λ_W(s) is
the boost preserving W_R. The modular conjugation J_W is the CPT operator composed with a
rotation by π about the x¹ axis.

Bisognano and Wichmann (1975–76) proved it for nets generated by Wightman fields, using
analytic continuation of the boosts in complex rapidity and the PCT theorem.

In the pure Haag–Kastler setting BW is **not a consequence of the axioms**:
- Counterexamples exist. These are covariant nets with positive energy and a vacuum in which
  the wedge modular group is not the boost group. One is due to Yngvason ("A note on essential
  duality", Lett. Math. Phys. 1994); others are discussed by Borchers and by Morinelli.
- The usual failure mode: the net is covariant under more than one Poincaré representation, or
  carries an internal symmetry that mixes with the boosts. Δ^{it} and the boost then differ by
  a unitary group that commutes with the translations.

So a pure-AQFT formalization must choose between two goals:
- (a) prove the parts that do hold unconditionally;
- (b) prove BW under an extra hypothesis that is itself algebraic, not Wightman.

## 2. What holds unconditionally: Borchers' theorem and its wedge corollary

**Borchers' theorem** (Borchers, CMP 143, 1992; simpler proofs by Florig, J. Math. Phys. 1998,
and in Longo's lecture notes on standard subspaces). Let M be a von Neumann algebra with a
cyclic and separating vector Ω, and t ↦ V(t) = e^{itP} a unitary group with:
- P ≥ 0;
- V(t)Ω = Ω;
- V(t) M V(t)* ⊆ M for t ≥ 0.

Then Δ^{is} V(t) Δ^{−is} = V(e^{−2πs} t) and J V(t) J = V(−t).

It is pure operator algebra plus positive energy in a single direction.

**Wedge corollary** (Borchers 1992). Apply the theorem to M = R(W_R) and the two lightlike
half-line translations that map W_R into itself:
- along l₊ = (1,1) the generator is ≥ 0;
- along −l₋ = (−1,1) the generator is ≤ 0, so the sign of the scaling flips.

The transverse translations (x², x³) map W_R onto itself and fix Ω, so they commute with Δ_W^{it}.
Together these give, for every translation a:

  Δ_W^{it} U(a) Δ_W^{−it} = U(Λ_W(−2πt) a),   J_W U(a) J_W = U(j_W a).

Hence Z(t) := Δ_W^{it} U(Λ_W(2πt)) is a one-parameter unitary group that commutes with all
translations, fixes Ω and maps R(W_R) onto itself. **BW is exactly the statement Z ≡ 1.** Every
algebraic proof of BW is a way of killing Z.

## 3. Algebraic hypotheses under which BW is known (no Wightman fields)

| Route | Extra hypothesis | Size and fit |
|---|---|---|
| Modular covariance (Brunetti–Guido–Longo) | Δ_W^{it} acts geometrically for every wedge | BW is then nearly the hypothesis itself; not worth formalizing as "BW" |
| Kuckert's theorem (CMP 2002, "Two uniqueness results on the Unruh effect and on PCT symmetry") | J_W acts geometrically (modular reflection covariance), or uniqueness of the PCT/covariance representation | Moderate; a genuine theorem built on Borchers |
| Chiral/conformal nets (Brunetti–Guido–Longo 1993; Gabbiani–Fröhlich 1993) | Möbius covariance on S¹ with positive energy; BW then holds unconditionally | A different framework (nets on S¹), outside the Minkowski chapter 10 setting |
| Massive particle theories (Mund, Ann. Henri Poincaré 2001) | Mass gap, massive one-particle states, and asymptotic completeness for the full statement | Very large: Haag–Ruelle scattering theory first |
| Geometric modular action (Buchholz–Dreyer–Florig–Summers) | The modular conjugations form a group realising the wedge reflections | The reverse direction (geometry from modular data); not BW as stated |

The cheapest honest target is therefore **Borchers' theorem plus the wedge corollary**, which
reduces BW to Z ≡ 1. A **conditional BW** can then follow: Z ≡ 1 under a stated algebraic
hypothesis, Kuckert-style or a uniqueness-of-covariance assumption. The hypothesis would be
recorded under a `**Restriction:**` line or as an explicit binder.

## 4. What the library has, and what is missing

**Available:**
- **Modular theory:** Tomita's theorem, Δ^{it} as `modularUnitaryGroup`, J, Takesaki's KMS
  theorem, J Δ^{it} = Δ^{it} J, Δ^{it}Ω = Ω, and the KMS-function machinery (bounded functions
  of Δ, cut-off approximants, strip analyticity).
- **Positive energy and Stone:** `IsPositiveEnergy V` (strongly continuous, positive generator);
  Stone's theorem with an unbounded generator, `isPositiveEnergy_iff_exists_exp`.
- **Holomorphic extension:** `IsPositiveEnergy.exists_holomorphic_extension` (Reeh–Schlieder),
  giving t ↦ ⟨ξ, V(t)η⟩ holomorphic in the upper half-plane.
- **Edge-of-the-wedge and uniqueness lemmas:** `EdgeVanishing.lean` and
  `HorizontalLineRemovable.lean`.
- **Reeh–Schlieder:** cyclicity and separation of Ω for diamond algebras.
- **Net structure:** Lorentz covariance as `InhomogeneousLorentzGroup` acting through β_L on the
  quasilocal algebra; one-parameter subgroups (`IsOneParameterSubgroup`); the vacuum corollary
  with unitary implementation.

**Missing:**
1. **Wedge regions and wedge algebras.** The region classes are diamonds only, and the library
   attaches no algebra to unbounded regions; that is a recorded design decision (Reeh–Schlieder
   "additive-free locality").
   - The natural definition is R(W) := (⋃_{B ⊆ W diamond} R(B))″. It needs a new `def` or
     region class: a **design decision for the user**.
   - Then: R(W) is a von Neumann algebra; Ω is cyclic for it (Reeh–Schlieder, since W contains
     diamonds); Ω is separating for it (Ω is cyclic for R(W′) and R(W′) ⊆ R(W)′ by locality).
2. **Unitary implementation of the Poincaré group in the vacuum representation**, as a family
   U(Λ, a) with U(a)R(B)U(a)* = R(B + a) and positive energy along lightlike directions. Partial
   pieces exist (implementation of one-parameter subgroups, positive energy one direction at a
   time). The wedge corollary needs the boost subgroup and the lightlike translation subgroups,
   with the commutation U(Λ)U(a)U(Λ)* = U(Λa).
3. **Borchers' theorem itself**, about 30–50 blueprint nodes. In outline (Florig's or Longo's
   version):
   - For ξ, η in a dense set, consider F(s) = ⟨ξ, Δ^{−is} V(t) Δ^{is} η⟩. Holomorphy of the
     two-variable function z, w ↦ ⟨Δ^{…} ξ, V(w) …⟩ comes from positive energy (upper
     half-plane in w) and from the KMS / strip analyticity of Δ^{iz} on MΩ (strip
     0 ≤ Im z ≤ 1/2).
   - Use V(t)MV(t)* ⊆ M (t ≥ 0) to show that the boundary values match after the substitution
     w ↦ e^{2πz} w.
   - Conclude with a uniqueness theorem: a bounded holomorphic function on a strip or half-plane
     with matching boundary values (Schwarz reflection / edge of the wedge, or a Liouville
     argument).
   - Most ingredients exist in some form: strip formula, KMS functions, holomorphic extension of
     positive-energy groups, edge-vanishing. The genuinely new analysis is the two-variable
     argument, and controlling analytic vectors of Δ on the translated algebra.
4. **A notion of the group generated by the translations and the boosts in a lightlike plane**,
   to assemble the two Borchers relations and the transverse invariance into
   Δ^{it}U(a)Δ^{−it} = U(Λ(−2πt)a).
5. **For a conditional BW:** the chosen extra hypothesis, and an argument that it forces Z ≡ 1.
   - Under a Kuckert-type hypothesis, the argument uses J_W Z(t) J_W = Z(t) together with the
     commutation of Z with the translations. Its formal content and size need a second scouting
     pass on Kuckert's paper.
   - Under "uniqueness of the covariant representation" the argument is short but the
     hypothesis is strong.

## 5. Rough plan and order (if pursued)

1. **Design decisions (user):**
   - a wedge region and the wedge algebra R(W);
   - the unitary Poincaré implementation in the vacuum representation, as an axiom-structure
     field or a hypothesis;
   - which extra hypothesis, if any, a conditional BW uses.
2. **Wedge algebra basics:** von Neumann algebra; Ω cyclic and separating; covariance
   U(g)R(W)U(g)* = R(gW). Small, about 10–15 nodes.
3. **Borchers' theorem** as pure operator algebra in `Physicslib4/Operators/Modular/`. It is
   independent of nets, reuses the Tomita/Takesaki infrastructure, and is a self-contained
   milestone. About 30–50 nodes.
4. **Wedge corollary** (Borchers 1992 Thm II): commutation of Δ_W^{it} and J_W with all
   translations, and the definition of Z(t). About 10–20 nodes.
5. **Conditional BW**, after a further scouting pass on Kuckert 2002 and Morinelli 2018 to pick
   the hypothesis and size the proof.

Whether **unconditional** BW follows from additional standard assumptions (for example a mass
gap with asymptotic completeness, following Mund) is a research-sized formalization: Haag–Ruelle
scattering theory alone is far larger than everything in §10.10.

## 6. Recommendation

- Treat **Borchers' theorem** as the next modular-theory milestone. It is the AQFT-native core
  of BW, needs no new net axioms, and makes strong use of what §10.10 already provides.
- Decide the wedge design question separately, before the wedge corollary.
- Do not state an unconditional BW for Haag–Kastler nets: it is false in general.
