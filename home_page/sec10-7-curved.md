# §10.7 Haag–Kastler Axioms in curved spacetime (pp. 332–344)

52 items, Definitions 645–696. An abstract Lorentzian spacetime interface (Definition 645) supplies what the curved axioms use—points, basis sets, complete spacelike separation, and an isometry group acting on points and preserving basis sets—and every geometric Lorentzian spacetime is an instance of it through the concrete bridge on the oriented identity component of its isometry group. Over it the axioms are restated for a Lorentzian spacetime: Local Algebras (Definition 646), Isotony (Definition 647, again supplying the family as chosen data with identity and composition laws), Local Commutativity (Definition 648, which uses the Axiom 2 family rather than choosing its own witnesses), local observables (Definition 649), Local Completeness (Definition 650), and Isometric Covariance (Definition 651, whose coherence condition, as in Minkowski, is stated for the Axiom 2 isotony family), bundled into a `HaagKastlerNet` in curved spacetime (Definition 652), and the axioms are jointly satisfiable (Theorem 653). This form of Axioms 2 and 3 has a visible consequence downstream: statements about nested regions have to factor a three-fold inclusion $$\mathbf{B}_1 \subseteq \mathbf{B}_2 \subseteq \mathbf{B}$$ inside a common containing algebra, and that factorisation is the composition law of Axiom 2, so **no coherence hypothesis is needed at any such site**—curved isotony (Theorem 659) and the curved von Neumann net (Definition 661) are unconditional. An explicit hypothesis remains only where the abstract interface genuinely cannot supply it, namely basis-set preservation in Geometric Covariance (Theorem 665), discharged for nets arising from a concrete geometric spacetime; the coherence of the stabiliser action with the isotony embeddings is condition (3) of Axiom 5 transported along $$g \cdot \mathbf{B} = \mathbf{B}$$.

- *§10.7.1 Einstein Causality in Curved Spacetime (p. 336, items 654–655).* The operator form of local commutativity, expressed in a representation of a common containing local algebra rather than of a global quasilocal algebra (Theorem 654), and its restriction to subregions of completely spacelike-separated regions on a concrete spacetime (Lemma 655).
- *§10.7.2 Local von Neumann Algebras in Curved Spacetime (p. 336, items 656–667).* The Minkowski development of §10.6.5 and §10.6.6 mirrored relative to a containing region: the local von Neumann algebra of a subregion and its bundled registration (Definitions 656–657), Microcausality (Theorem 658), unconditional Isotony (Theorem 659), the bundled form (Theorem 660), the net as an order-preserving map on the poset of subregions (Definition 661), Statistical Independence in set-level and bundled forms (Theorems 662–663), additive-free locality (Theorem 664), and Geometric Covariance via the stabiliser GNS representation (Theorem 665) with orbit-invariance of factoriality (Theorem 666) and the upgrade to a \*-algebra isomorphism (Theorem 667).
- *§10.7.3 Relative Commutants of Nested Local Algebras in Curved Spacetime (p. 338, items 668–676).* The curved counterpart of §10.6.7: the relative commutant of a nested pair inside a containing region (Definition 668), its containment in the larger algebra, commutation with the smaller, and containment of the centre (Theorems 669–671), irreducible inclusions (Definition 672) and their consequences (Theorems 673–674), plus the abelian/centre facts specialised to curved local algebras (Theorems 675–676).
- *§10.7.4 Purity of States on Local Algebras in Curved Spacetime (p. 340, items 677–681).* Each local algebra is a unital C\*-algebra with its own state space, so the abstract purity characterisations are registered per region: Pure $$\iff$$ Extreme Point (Theorem 677), Pure $$\iff$$ Irreducible GNS (Theorem 678), the GNS representation of a pure state generates a factor and indeed all of $$\mathcal{B}(H)$$ (Theorem 679), the Irreducible Dichotomy for a curved local algebra (Theorem 680), and GNS covariance for curved local algebras (Theorem 681).
- *§10.7.5 Covariant States in Curved Spacetime (p. 340, items 682–683).* Covariant families of local states in curved spacetime (Definition 682) and their composition law (Lemma 683).
- *§10.7.6 The Stabilizer GNS Unitary in Curved Spacetime (p. 341, items 684–690).* Since no quasilocal algebra exists on a generic Lorentzian spacetime, the covariance action restricts to the stabiliser subgroup $$\mathrm{Stab}(\mathbf{B})$$ of a region, giving automorphisms of the single local algebra $$\mathfrak{U}(\mathbf{B})$$ (Definition 684) that form a genuine group action (Lemma 685). A stabiliser-invariant state carries a unitary GNS representation of $$\mathrm{Stab}(\mathbf{B})$$ (Theorem 686), strongly continuous when the matrix coefficients are continuous (Theorem 687); a state both stabiliser-invariant and pure yields an irreducible covariant representation (Theorem 688); purity is invariant under the stabiliser action (Theorem 689), and GNS data transports along it (Theorem 690).
- *§10.7.7 KMS States for a Killing Flow (p. 342, items 691–696).* Killing flows are identified as one-parameter subgroups of the stabiliser of a region, inducing a one-parameter automorphism group on $$\mathfrak{U}(\mathbf{B})$$ (Definition 691, Lemma 692). KMS states for a Killing flow (Definition 693) are the precise algebraic sense in which the Hartle–Hawking and Gibbons–Hawking states are thermal. Such a state at positive inverse temperature automatically carries a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group on its GNS Hilbert space implementing the flow, yielding the curved-spacetime thermal representation—the analogue of the Minkowski vacuum representation (Theorem 694); these states form a convex set (Theorem 695), and the corresponding ground state is recorded alongside them (Definition 696).
