# §10.7 Haag–Kastler Axioms in curved spacetime (pp. 328–338)

49 items, Definitions 627–675. The axioms are restated for a Lorentzian spacetime: Local Algebras (Definition 627), Isotony (Definition 628, again supplying the family as chosen data with identity and composition laws), Local Commutativity (Definition 629, which uses the Axiom 2 family rather than choosing its own witnesses), local observables (Definition 630), Local Completeness (Definition 631), and Isometric Covariance (Definition 632, whose coherence condition, as in Minkowski, is stated for the Axiom 2 isotony family), bundled into a `HaagKastlerNet` in curved spacetime (Definition 633). This form of Axioms 2 and 3 has a visible consequence downstream: statements about nested regions have to factor a three-fold inclusion $$\mathbf{B}_1 \subseteq \mathbf{B}_2 \subseteq \mathbf{B}$$ inside a common containing algebra, and that factorisation is the composition law of Axiom 2, so **no coherence hypothesis is needed at any such site**—curved isotony (Theorem 638) and the curved von Neumann net (Definition 640) are unconditional. An explicit hypothesis remains only where the abstract interface genuinely cannot supply it, namely basis-set preservation in Geometric Covariance (Theorem 644), discharged for nets arising from a concrete geometric spacetime; the coherence of the stabiliser action with the isotony embeddings is condition (3) of Axiom 5 transported along $$g \cdot \mathbf{B} = \mathbf{B}$$.

- *§10.7.1 Einstein Causality in Curved Spacetime (p. 331, item 634).* The operator form of local commutativity, expressed in a representation of a common containing local algebra rather than of a global quasilocal algebra (Theorem 634).
- *§10.7.2 Local von Neumann Algebras in Curved Spacetime (p. 331, items 635–646).* The Minkowski development of §10.6.5 mirrored relative to a containing region: the local von Neumann algebra of a subregion and its bundled registration (Definitions 635–636), Microcausality (Theorem 637), unconditional Isotony (Theorem 638), the bundled form (Theorem 639), the net as an order-preserving map on the poset of subregions (Definition 640), Statistical Independence in set-level and bundled forms (Theorems 641–642), additive-free locality (Theorem 643), and Geometric Covariance via the stabiliser GNS representation (Theorem 644) with orbit-invariance of factoriality (Theorem 645) and the upgrade to a \*-algebra isomorphism (Theorem 646).
- *§10.7.3 Relative Commutants of Nested Local Algebras in Curved Spacetime (p. 333, items 647–655).* The curved counterpart of §10.6.6: the relative commutant of a nested pair inside a containing region (Definition 647), its containment in the larger algebra, commutation with the smaller, and containment of the centre (Theorems 648–650), irreducible inclusions (Definition 651) and their consequences (Theorems 652–653), plus the abelian/centre facts specialised to curved local algebras (Theorems 654–655).
- *§10.7.4 Purity of States on Local Algebras in Curved Spacetime (p. 334, items 656–660).* Each local algebra is a unital C\*-algebra with its own state space, so the abstract purity characterisations are registered per region: Pure $$\iff$$ Extreme Point (Theorem 656), Pure $$\iff$$ Irreducible GNS (Theorem 657), the GNS representation of a pure state generates a factor and indeed all of $$\mathcal{B}(H)$$ (Theorem 658), the Irreducible Dichotomy for a curved local algebra (Theorem 659), and GNS covariance for curved local algebras (Theorem 660).
- *§10.7.5 Covariant States in Curved Spacetime (p. 335, items 661–662).* Covariant families of local states in curved spacetime (Definition 661) and their composition law (Lemma 662).
- *§10.7.6 The Stabilizer GNS Unitary in Curved Spacetime (p. 335, items 663–669).* Since no quasilocal algebra exists on a generic Lorentzian spacetime, the covariance action restricts to the stabiliser subgroup $$\mathrm{Stab}(\mathbf{B})$$ of a region, giving automorphisms of the single local algebra $$\mathfrak{U}(\mathbf{B})$$ (Definition 663) that form a genuine group action (Lemma 664). A stabiliser-invariant state carries a unitary GNS representation of $$\mathrm{Stab}(\mathbf{B})$$ (Theorem 665), strongly continuous when the matrix coefficients are continuous (Theorem 666); a state both stabiliser-invariant and pure yields an irreducible covariant representation (Theorem 667); purity is invariant under the stabiliser action (Theorem 668), and GNS data transports along it (Theorem 669).
- *§10.7.7 KMS States for a Killing Flow (p. 337, items 670–675).* Killing flows are identified as one-parameter subgroups of the stabiliser of a region, inducing a one-parameter automorphism group on $$\mathfrak{U}(\mathbf{B})$$ (Definition 670, Lemma 671). KMS states for a Killing flow (Definition 672) are the precise algebraic sense in which the Hartle–Hawking and Gibbons–Hawking states are thermal. Such a state at positive inverse temperature automatically carries a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group on its GNS Hilbert space implementing the flow, yielding the curved-spacetime thermal representation—the analogue of the Minkowski vacuum representation (Theorem 673); these states form a convex set (Theorem 674), and the corresponding ground state is recorded alongside them (Definition 675).
