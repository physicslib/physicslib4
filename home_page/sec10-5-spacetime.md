# §10.5 Spacetime and causal structure (pp. 249–286)

137 items (Definitions 330–466), building the entire causal and topological apparatus the axioms are indexed on. The trips underlying the chronological and causal relations $$\ll$$ and $$\prec$$ are genuine geodesics of the Levi-Civita connection of the metric, which the section constructs for an arbitrary pseudo-Riemannian metric on top of Mathlib's covariant derivatives (Mathlib itself has only a Riemannian Levi-Civita connection and no geodesics). It proceeds in nine layers.

- *Spacetime, tangent-vector causality, and curves (pp. 249–254, items 330–353).* Gives precise definitions of spacetime (Definition 330) and standard Minkowski spacetime (Definition 331); classifies tangent vectors as timelike, spacelike, or null (Definition 332) and proves the trichotomy (Lemma 333), the reverse Cauchy–Schwarz and reverse triangle inequalities for timelike vectors (Lemmas 334–335), and the cone geometry—orientation of pointing vectors, the sign lemma, definiteness of the spacelike complement of a timelike vector, and convexity of the cones (Lemmas 338–341)—alongside time orientations (Definition 336) and future- and past-pointing vectors (Definition 337). Then paths, curves, and oriented curves are defined as equivalence classes of paths up to reparametrisation (Definitions 342–347), with causal type and future/past orientation each shown well-defined on the quotient (Theorems 346 and 348) and a forgetful projection from oriented to unoriented curves (Theorem 349). Endpoints (Definition 350), shown to be well defined on curves and smooth curves (Lemma 351), come with two point-set lemmas—an extremal parameter lies in the frontier, and two endpoints force a compact parameter interval (Lemmas 352–353).
- *The Levi-Civita connection and geodesics (pp. 254–258, items 354–369).* A new layer. Pseudo-Riemannian metrics (Definition 354) are smooth, symmetric, non-degenerate families of bilinear forms, with no signature condition, so everything here applies to every spacetime. Covariant derivatives are Mathlib's `IsCovariantDerivativeOn`; metric compatibility and the Levi-Civita condition (compatible and torsion-free) are defined on top of them (Definitions 355–356). The musical isomorphism $$v \mapsto g_x(v, \cdot)$$ is a linear isomorphism at each point (Lemma 357); the smoothness of its inverse (Lemma 358) is stated and proved in the blueprint but is the one node of the layer not yet formalised, and nothing depends on it. The Koszul formula (Lemma 359), the tensoriality of the Koszul expression (Lemma 360), and the verification that the Koszul connection is a covariant derivative (Lemma 361) that is Levi-Civita (Lemma 362) give existence and uniqueness of the Levi-Civita connection (Theorem 363). Five lemmas about smooth paths—a function vanishing along a path has zero derivative along it, the covariant derivative along a curve is local, local vector fields globalise, a smooth path has a local left inverse, and its velocity extends to a smooth vector field (Lemmas 364–368)—make the geodesic condition chart-free, well defined and non-vacuous. A geodesic (Definition 369) is then an affinely parametrised smooth path $$\mu$$ with $$(\nabla_X X)(\mu(s)) = 0$$ at every interior parameter, for every smooth vector field $$X$$ extending the velocity near $$s$$.
- *Trips, futures and pasts (pp. 258–260, items 370–378).* Trips and causal trips (Definitions 370–371) are finite chains of future-oriented timelike (respectively causal) geodesic segments, geodesic in the sense of Definition 369, with matching past and future endpoints. Chronological and causal precedence are transitive (Theorem 372); the causality condition (Definition 373) makes them a strict partial order (Theorem 374); and chronological and causal futures and pasts (Definitions 375–376) come with their basic inclusion and monotonicity properties (Lemmas 377–378).
- *§10.5.1 Causal diamonds (p. 260, items 379–393).* Introduces the causal diamond $$J^+(p) \cap J^-(q)$$ and the chronological (Alexandrov) diamond $$I^+(p) \cap I^-(q)$$ (Definition 379), with the structural properties of the causal diamond—monotonicity under endpoint spread, causal convexity, and that nonemptiness forces $$p \prec q$$ (Lemma 380)—and the containment of chronological diamonds in causal ones, together with the identification of the Alexandrov basis as exactly the chronological diamonds (Theorem 381). It then develops spacelike separation of points and of regions (Definitions 382–383, Lemmas 384–385), the spacelike complement $$\mathbf{B}^\perp$$ (Definition 386) and its order structure—antitone, extensive on the double complement, with the triple complement collapsing, making complementation the Galois connection attached to the spacelike-separation relation (Lemma 387). The double complement is packaged as the causal closure operator (Definition 388, Lemma 389), whose fixed points are the causally complete regions (Definition 390). These form a complete lattice—meets are intersections, joins are causal closures of unions—on which the causal complement is an order-reversing involution (Theorem 391); the set-level De Morgan laws (Lemma 392) then lift to full binary and infinitary De Morgan laws on the lattice (Theorem 393). The blueprint is careful to record what does *not* hold: the full orthocomplement law $$\mathbf{B} \wedge \mathbf{B}^\perp = \bot$$ fails at this generality, because the trip-based causal relation is irreflexive and so a point is spacelike-separated from itself.
- *§10.5.2 Causal convexity (p. 264, items 394–396).* Defines a causally convex region as one containing every point causally between two of its own points (Definition 394), shows causal diamonds are causally convex (Lemma 395), and shows every spacelike complement—hence every causally complete region—is causally convex (Theorem 396).
- *§10.5.3 Causal convexity: closure structure, and the Alexandrov basis theorems (p. 265, items 397–420).* The causally convex regions are shown to form a closure system (Lemma 397), giving the causal-convex hull (Definition 398) as a genuine closure operator whose closed sets are exactly the causally convex regions (Lemmas 399, Theorem 400). The subsection then turns to the Alexandrov topology (Definition 401): every diamond is open (Lemma 402); chronological futures and pasts are open under an explicit "no endpoints" hypothesis (Lemma 403) and unconditionally on standard Minkowski spacetime, where the coordinate-cone description discharges the hypothesis (Lemma 404). On standard Minkowski spacetime the componentwise directional derivative is a Levi-Civita connection (Lemma 405), hence the Levi-Civita connection is flat (Lemma 406) and affinely parametrised straight lines are geodesics (Lemma 407), so trips there are the familiar straight-line segments. Minkowski spacetime and Lorentzian spacetime are then defined (Definitions 408–409, the latter as a spacetime whose Alexandrov topology is Hausdorff), with a bundled form of spacelike separation and basis openness (Lemma 410). A point lying in no diamond is pathological: its only Alexandrov neighbourhood is the whole space (Lemma 411), and the Hausdorff assumption rules this out, forcing the diamonds to cover the space (Lemma 412). Given downward-directedness the diamonds form a genuine topological basis (Theorem 413); past and future interpolation on standard Minkowski (Lemmas 414–415) supply downward-directedness there (Lemma 416), and common chronological predecessors and successors supply upward-directedness (Lemmas 417–419), so on standard Minkowski the diamonds are an unconditional basis (Theorem 420). Upward-directedness is what the quasilocal colimit of §10.6.1 later consumes.
- *§10.5.4 Dilations are causal automorphisms but not isometries (p. 271, items 421–423).* The dilations $$x \mapsto \lambda x$$ preserve the Minkowski cones (Lemma 421) and are therefore causal automorphisms (Theorem 422), yet scale the metric by $$\lambda^2$$ and so are not isometries for $$\lambda \neq 1$$ (Theorem 423). This is the counterexample that forces the general-covariance morphism of §10.8 to be specified geometrically rather than causally.
- *§10.5.5 Isometries and basis-set preservation (p. 272, items 424–434).* Single-metric transport: isometries preserve the causal classification of tangent vectors (Lemma 424), paths have unique differentials and well-defined pushforwards (Lemmas 425–426), and isometries respect the Levi-Civita structure: pullbacks of vector fields and of the metric pairing, their derivatives and Lie brackets transform naturally (Lemmas 427–429), so an isometry preserves the Levi-Civita connection (Lemma 430) and maps geodesics to geodesics and back (Lemma 431). Future-orientation-preserving isometries preserve chronology (Lemma 432) and map Alexandrov-basis diamonds to diamonds (Lemma 433), which is exactly the well-definedness condition for the Axiom 5 action (Lemma 434).
- *§10.5.6 Pullback metrics and cross-metric isometries (p. 274, items 435–466).* The single-metric lemmas above compare a spacetime with itself; general covariance instead compares two different metrics on one carrier, so the transport statements are redone cross-metric. This subsection defines the pullback $$\psi^* g$$ of a spacetime metric as a bundled family of continuous bilinear forms (Definition 435) and verifies every obligation in turn: the differential of a diffeomorphism is a linear equivalence (Lemma 436), with the two round-trip cancellation identities that Mathlib does not supply for a global `Diffeomorph` proved by hand (Lemmas 437–439); the pullback metric is symmetric, non-degenerate, Lorentzian, and a smooth section of the bilinear-form bundle (Lemmas 440–443), so the pullback of a spacetime is a spacetime (Theorem 444). Two-sided preservation of future orientation is then defined (Definition 445) and the pullback time orientation is shown to be bundle-smooth, nowhere vanishing, and everywhere timelike (Lemmas 446–449), with transport of future-pointing timelike and null vectors (Lemmas 450–451) giving two-sided orientation preservation (Lemma 452). Cross-metric isometries are defined (Definition 453) and shown closed under inverses (Lemma 454), to preserve causal classification (Lemma 455), to push paths forward preserving the timelike/causal conditions and endpoints (Lemmas 456–459), and to transport chronological precedence and the chronological future and past (Lemmas 460–462), hence to preserve Alexandrov-basis sets (Lemma 463). A general topological lemma—a bijection matching generating families is a homeomorphism (Lemma 464)—then identifies the pullback Alexandrov topology (Lemma 465) and yields that the pullback of a Lorentzian spacetime is again a Lorentzian spacetime (Theorem 466). Without Theorem 466 the phrase "the net over $$\psi^*(M,g)$$" in §10.8 would have no referent.
