# §10.5 Spacetime and causal structure (pp. 250–289)

146 items (Definitions 330–475), building the entire causal and topological apparatus the axioms are indexed on. The trips underlying the chronological and causal relations $$\ll$$ and $$\prec$$ are genuine geodesics of the Levi-Civita connection of the metric, which the section constructs for an arbitrary pseudo-Riemannian metric on top of Mathlib's covariant derivatives. It proceeds in nine layers.

- *Spacetime, tangent-vector causality, and curves (pp. 250–256, items 330–357).* Gives precise definitions of spacetime (Definition 330) and standard Minkowski spacetime (Definition 331); classifies tangent vectors as timelike, spacelike, or null (Definition 332) and proves the trichotomy (Lemma 333), the reverse Cauchy–Schwarz and reverse triangle inequalities for timelike vectors (Lemmas 334–335), and the cone geometry—orientation of pointing vectors, the sign lemma, definiteness of the spacelike complement of a timelike vector, and convexity of the cones (Lemmas 342–345)—alongside time orientations (Definition 340) and future- and past-pointing vectors (Definition 341). The Lorentz, proper and orthochronous transformations of standard Minkowski spacetime are defined (Definition 336); orthochronous Lorentz transformations preserve the future cone (Lemma 337), the identity is Lorentz, proper and orthochronous and each condition is closed under composition and inversion, so that $$SO(1,3)^{\uparrow}$$ is a group (Lemma 338), and the inhomogeneous Lorentz group is built on top of it (Definition 339). Then paths, curves, and oriented curves are defined as equivalence classes of paths up to reparametrisation (Definitions 346–351), with causal type and future/past orientation each shown well-defined on the quotient (Theorems 350 and 352) and a forgetful projection from oriented to unoriented curves (Theorem 353). Endpoints (Definition 354), shown to be well defined on curves and smooth curves (Lemma 355), come with two point-set lemmas—an extremal parameter lies in the frontier, and two endpoints force a compact parameter interval (Lemmas 356–357).
- *The Levi-Civita connection and geodesics (pp. 256–260, items 358–373).* Pseudo-Riemannian metrics (Definition 358) are smooth, symmetric, non-degenerate families of bilinear forms, with no signature condition, so everything here applies to every spacetime. Covariant derivatives are Mathlib's `IsCovariantDerivativeOn`; metric compatibility and the Levi-Civita condition (compatible and torsion-free) are defined on top of them (Definitions 359–360). The musical isomorphism $$v \mapsto g_x(v, \cdot)$$ is a linear isomorphism at each point (Lemma 361), and its inverse is a smooth section of $$\mathrm{Hom}(T^*M, TM)$$ (Lemma 362). The Koszul formula (Lemma 363), the tensoriality of the Koszul expression (Lemma 364), and the verification that the Koszul connection is a covariant derivative (Lemma 365) that is Levi-Civita (Lemma 366) give existence and uniqueness of the Levi-Civita connection (Theorem 367). Five lemmas about smooth paths—a function vanishing along a path has zero derivative along it, the covariant derivative along a curve is local, local vector fields globalise, a smooth path has a local left inverse, and its velocity extends to a smooth vector field (Lemmas 368–372)—make the geodesic condition chart-free, well defined and non-vacuous. A geodesic (Definition 373) is then an affinely parametrised smooth path $$\mu$$ in a spacetime with $$(\nabla_X X)(\mu(s)) = 0$$ at every interior parameter, for every smooth vector field $$X$$ extending the velocity near $$s$$.
- *Trips, futures and pasts (pp. 260–262, items 374–382).* Trips and causal trips (Definitions 374–375) are finite chains of future-oriented timelike (respectively causal) geodesic segments, geodesic in the sense of Definition 373, with matching past and future endpoints; causal precedence $$p \prec q$$ means $$p = q$$ or a causal trip from $$p$$ to $$q$$, so it is reflexive, as in Hawking–Ellis. Chronological and causal precedence are transitive (Theorem 376); under the causality condition that no causal trip runs from a point to itself (Definition 377), chronological precedence is irreflexive, causal trips are asymmetric and causal precedence is a partial order (Theorem 378); and chronological and causal futures and pasts (Definitions 379–380) come with their basic inclusion and monotonicity properties (Lemmas 381–382).
- *§10.5.1 Causal diamonds (p. 262, items 383–398).* Introduces the causal diamond $$J^+(p) \cap J^-(q)$$ and the chronological (Alexandrov) diamond $$I^+(p) \cap I^-(q)$$ (Definition 383), with the structural properties of the causal diamond—monotonicity under endpoint spread, causal convexity, and that nonemptiness forces $$p \prec q$$ (Lemma 384)—and the containment of chronological diamonds in causal ones, together with the identification of the Alexandrov basis as exactly the chronological diamonds (Theorem 385). It then develops spacelike separation of points and of regions (Definitions 386–387, Lemmas 388–389), shows that any map preserving causal precedence in both directions preserves spacelike separation of points and complete spacelike separation of regions (Lemma 390), and introduces the spacelike complement $$\mathbf{B}^\perp$$ (Definition 391) and its order structure—antitone, extensive on the double complement, with the triple complement collapsing, making complementation the Galois connection attached to the spacelike-separation relation (Lemma 392). The double complement is packaged as the causal closure operator (Definition 393, Lemma 394), whose fixed points are the causally complete regions (Definition 395). These form a complete lattice—meets are intersections, joins are causal closures of unions—on which the causal complement is an order-reversing involution and an orthocomplement, $$\mathbf{B} \wedge \mathbf{B}^\perp = \bot$$ and $$\mathbf{B} \vee \mathbf{B}^\perp = \top$$ (Theorem 397); the set-level De Morgan laws (Lemma 396) then lift to full binary and infinitary De Morgan laws on the lattice (Theorem 398). Orthocomplementation rests on the fact that no point is spacelike related to itself (Definition 386), so that a region and its spacelike complement are disjoint (Definition 391).
- *§10.5.2 Causal convexity (p. 267, items 399–401).* Defines a causally convex region as one containing every point causally between two of its own points (Definition 399), shows causal diamonds are causally convex (Lemma 400), and shows every spacelike complement—hence every causally complete region—is causally convex (Theorem 401).
- *§10.5.3 Causal convexity: closure structure, and the Alexandrov basis theorems (p. 267, items 402–425).* The causally convex regions are shown to form a closure system (Lemma 402), giving the causal-convex hull (Definition 403) as a genuine closure operator whose closed sets are exactly the causally convex regions (Lemmas 404, Theorem 405). The subsection then turns to the Alexandrov topology (Definition 406): every diamond is open (Lemma 407); chronological futures and pasts are open under an explicit "no endpoints" hypothesis (Lemma 408) and unconditionally on standard Minkowski spacetime, where the coordinate-cone description discharges the hypothesis (Lemma 409). On standard Minkowski spacetime the componentwise directional derivative is a Levi-Civita connection (Lemma 410), hence the Levi-Civita connection is flat (Lemma 411) and affinely parametrised straight lines are geodesics (Lemma 412), so trips there are the familiar straight-line segments. Minkowski spacetime and Lorentzian spacetime are then defined (Definitions 413–414, the latter as a spacetime whose Alexandrov topology is Hausdorff), with a bundled form of spacelike separation and basis openness (Lemma 415). A point lying in no diamond is pathological: its only Alexandrov neighbourhood is the whole space (Lemma 416), and the Hausdorff assumption rules this out, forcing the diamonds to cover the space (Lemma 417). Given downward-directedness the diamonds form a genuine topological basis (Theorem 418); past and future interpolation on standard Minkowski (Lemmas 419–420) supply downward-directedness there (Lemma 421), and common chronological predecessors and successors supply upward-directedness (Lemmas 422–424), so on standard Minkowski the diamonds are an unconditional basis (Theorem 425). Upward-directedness is what the quasilocal colimit of §10.6.1 later consumes.
- *§10.5.4 Dilations are causal automorphisms but not isometries (p. 274, items 426–428).* The dilations $$x \mapsto \lambda x$$ preserve the Minkowski cones (Lemma 426) and are therefore causal automorphisms (Theorem 427), yet scale the metric by $$\lambda^2$$ and so are not isometries for $$\lambda \neq 1$$ (Theorem 428). This is the counterexample that forces the general-covariance morphism of §10.8 to be specified geometrically rather than causally.
- *§10.5.5 Isometries and basis-set preservation (p. 274, items 429–443).* Single-metric transport: isometries preserve the causal classification of tangent vectors (Lemma 429), paths have unique differentials and well-defined pushforwards (Lemmas 430–431), and isometries respect the Levi-Civita structure: pullbacks of vector fields and of the metric pairing, their derivatives and Lie brackets transform naturally (Lemmas 432–434), so an isometry preserves the Levi-Civita connection (Lemma 435) and maps geodesics to geodesics and back (Lemma 436). The inhomogeneous Lorentz group acts on smooth paths by pushforward (Definition 437), carrying future-oriented causal paths to future-oriented causal paths (Lemma 438), so causal precedence (Theorem 439) and spacelike separation (Corollary 440) are Lorentz invariant. Future-orientation-preserving isometries preserve chronology (Lemma 441) and map Alexandrov-basis diamonds to diamonds (Lemma 442), which is exactly the well-definedness condition for the Axiom 5 action (Lemma 443).
- *§10.5.6 Pullback metrics and cross-metric isometries (p. 277, items 444–475).* The single-metric lemmas above compare a spacetime with itself; general covariance instead compares two different metrics on one carrier, so the transport statements are redone cross-metric. This subsection defines the pullback $$\psi^* g$$ of a spacetime metric as a bundled family of continuous bilinear forms (Definition 444) and verifies every obligation in turn: the differential of a diffeomorphism is a linear equivalence (Lemma 445), with the two round-trip cancellation identities for a global `Diffeomorph` (Lemmas 446–448); the pullback metric is symmetric, non-degenerate, Lorentzian, and a smooth section of the bilinear-form bundle (Lemmas 449–452), so the pullback of a spacetime is a spacetime (Theorem 453). Two-sided preservation of future orientation is then defined (Definition 454) and the pullback time orientation is shown to be bundle-smooth, nowhere vanishing, and everywhere timelike (Lemmas 455–458), with transport of future-pointing timelike and null vectors (Lemmas 459–460) giving two-sided orientation preservation (Lemma 461). Cross-metric isometries are defined (Definition 462) and shown closed under inverses (Lemma 463), to preserve causal classification (Lemma 464), to push paths forward preserving the timelike/causal conditions and endpoints (Lemmas 465–468), and to transport chronological precedence and the chronological future and past (Lemmas 469–471), hence to preserve Alexandrov-basis sets (Lemma 472). A general topological lemma—a bijection matching generating families is a homeomorphism (Lemma 473)—then identifies the pullback Alexandrov topology (Lemma 474) and yields that the pullback of a Lorentzian spacetime is again a Lorentzian spacetime (Theorem 475). Without Theorem 475 the phrase "the net over $$\psi^*(M,g)$$" in §10.8 would have no referent.
